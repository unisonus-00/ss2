# Stage 12: Vākya — Free Composition

## Goal
Compose freely in Sanskrit — describe daily life, thoughts, and experiences. Begin thinking directly in Sanskrit without translating from English.

## The Shift: From Grammar to Thought

Stages 1–9 gave you building blocks. Now you combine them fluidly. This stage is about **automaticity** — moving from conscious rule-application to natural expression.

## Sentence Patterns for Daily Life

### Pattern 1: Simple Statement (Subject + Verb)
> ahaṃ paṭhāmi — I study
> sūryaḥ udeti — the sun rises
> vṛṣṭiḥ bhavati — rain happens

### Pattern 2: Transitive (Subject + Object + Verb)
> ahaṃ pustakam paṭhāmi — I read a book
> mātā bhojanaṃ pacati — mother cooks food
> bālaḥ jalami pibati — the child drinks water

### Pattern 3: Full Sentence (S + Instrument + Location + Object + Verb)
> ahaṃ prātaḥ gṛhe bhojanaṃ karomi — I eat food at home in the morning

### Pattern 4: Compound Sentence (X ca Y ca)
> ahaṃ paṭhāmi likhāmi ca — I study and write
> sūryaḥ udeti candraḥ ca astam gacchati — the sun rises and the moon sets

### Pattern 5: Relative Clause (yaḥ... saḥ...)
> yaḥ bhajati saḥ mucyate — he who worships is liberated
> yā devī sarva-bhūteṣu... — she who is the goddess in all beings...

## The Game: "Tell Bhagavān About Your Day"

Speak to your iṣṭadevatā about your day in simple Sanskrit:

> he śiva!
> adya prātaḥ aham uttiṣṭhāmi.
> snānaṃ karomi.
> tava mandiraṃ gacchāmi.
> tatra tvāṃ paśyāmi.
> puṣpam arpayāmi.
> gṛhaṃ āgacchāmi.
> pustakam paṭhāmi.
> sāyam bhojanaṃ karomi.
> rātrau tvāṃ smarāmi.
> sukhaṃ svapimi.

## Building Longer Passages

### Describing a Place
> mama gṛham sundaram asti. tatra ekaṃ mandiram asti. mandire śivasya mūrtiḥ asti. pratidinṃ ahaṃ tatra pūjāṃ karomi. mandiraṃ śāntam asti. tatra ahaṃ dhyānaṃ karomi.

### Describing a Person
> mama guruḥ mahān asti. saḥ śāntaḥ asti. saḥ jñānī asti. saḥ pradinaṃ vedān paṭhati. saḥ śiṣyān pāṭhayati. ahaṃ tasya śiṣyaḥ asmi.

### Narrating an Event
> hyaḥ ahaṃ mandiraṃ gacchāmi (agaccham). tatra mahāpūjā āsīt. bahavaḥ bhaktāḥ āsan. sarve militvā stotram agāyan. atiśobhanam āsīt.

## Thinking in Sanskrit — Tips

1. **Start with objects around you** — name them in Sanskrit
2. **Narrate your actions** as you do them: uttiṣṭhāmi, gacchāmi...
3. **Use simple sentences first**, then add cases and qualifiers
4. **Don't translate from English** — think in Subject-Object-Verb order
5. **Talk to your deity** daily in Sanskrit — even one sentence

## Milestone Check

Can you speak for 2 minutes in simple Sanskrit about your day, your surroundings, or your devotional practice — without pausing to translate from English?
