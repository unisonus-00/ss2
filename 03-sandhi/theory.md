# Stage 3: Sandhi — The Snap-Rules

## Why This Matters

Every mantra you chant, every stotra you recite, every śloka you read aloud — sandhi is already happening in your mouth. When you say "namaḥ śivāya," the visarga of namaḥ transforms before the ś. When you chant "sadāśiva," two words have fused into one. Sandhi is not abstract grammar — it is the physics of sacred sound, the rules your voice already follows when joining syllables in worship.

Master these rules and you can **read any Sanskrit text on sight**, split compounds back into their components, and understand why mantras sound the way they do. Without sandhi, Sanskrit remains a collection of isolated words. With it, the words flow into living speech.

## Goal

Understand and apply the core sandhi rules of Sanskrit. Given any two words or morphemes, identify which sandhi applies, perform the combination, and split compound forms back into their components.

## What is Sandhi?

**Sandhi** (सन्धि) means "joining" or "junction." In the LEGO framework, these are the **snap-rules** — the laws governing what happens when two bricks click together. Just as LEGO bricks have studs and tubes that must align, Sanskrit sounds have contact rules that transform letters at the boundary. These changes are predictable, governed by Pāṇini's sūtras.

### Three Types of Sound Change (Kārya)

| Type | Sanskrit | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| Ādeśa | आदेश | Replacement -- a new sound replaces the old |
| Āgama | आगम | Insertion -- a new sound is added alongside existing sounds |
| Lopa | लोप | Deletion -- an existing sound disappears |

### Where Sandhi Applies

| Context | Mandatory? |
|---------|-----------|
| Prakṛti + Pratyaya (root + suffix, inside a word) | Yes (nitya) |
| Upasarga + Dhātu (prefix + verb root) | Yes (nitya) |
| Samāsa (compound words) | Yes (nitya) |
| Vākya (between words in a sentence) | Optional (vikalpa) |

### Utsarga and Apavāda — General Rule and Exception

Two Pāṇinian concepts that decide *which* sandhi fires when several could apply:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Utsarga** (उत्सर्ग) | The general rule — what happens by default. |
| **Apavāda** (अपवाद) | A specific exception that overrides the general rule for its own narrower scope. |

When an apavāda's condition holds, it wins. Everywhere else, utsarga fires. Example: Savarṇa-dīrgha ("same-class vowels merge to dīrgha") is the general rule for two similar vowels; Guṇa ("a/ā + i/ī → e") is the apavāda that takes over when the vowels are dissimilar in the specific a-class + i-class direction — the two rules never fight because their conditions do not overlap. Almost every sandhi you meet is either an utsarga or an apavāda to some broader rule.

---

## Classification of Sandhi

Sandhis are classified by which type of sound undergoes transformation:

| Category | Also Called | What Changes | Count in This Course |
|----------|-----------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Svara Sandhi** (vowel) | Ac Sandhi | Svaras (vowels) | 7 |
| **Vyañjana Sandhi** (consonant) | Hal Sandhi | Vyañjanas (consonants) | 12 |
| **Viśeṣa Sandhi** (special/visarga) | Visarga Sandhi | Visarga and special cases | 8 |

The pratyāhāra **ac** (from the Māheśvara Sūtras) represents all svaras, hence "ac sandhi." Similarly, **hal** represents all vyañjanas.

# Part I: Ac Sandhi (Svara / Vowel Sandhi)

## 1. Yan Sandhi (यण् सन्धि)

### Rule

When **i/ī, u/ū, ṛ/ṝ, or ḷ** occur at the end of the first component and are followed by any **dissimilar vowel** (asavarṇa svara), they are replaced by the corresponding semivowel.

### Sūtra

> iko yaṇ aci (इको यणचि) -- Pāṇini 6.1.77

### Table

| Pūrva (left) | Para (right) | Ādeśa (result) |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| i / ī | any svara except i/ī | y |
| u / ū | any svara except u/ū | v |
| ṛ / ṝ | any svara except ṛ/ṝ | r |
| ḷ | any svara except ḷ | l |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| prati + ekam | pratyekam | i + e → y + e |
| su + āgatam | svāgatam | u + ā → v + ā |
| pitṛ + ājñā | pitrājñā | ṛ + ā → r + ā |
| devī + arpitam | devyarpitam | ī + a → y + a |
| madhu + ariḥ | madhvariḥ | u + a → v + a |

### Key Points

- The ādeśa happens only to the pūrva (left-side) vowel.
- The para (right-side) vowel remains unchanged; it is the *nimitta* (cause) but does not itself transform.
- The semivowel shares the same sthāna (place of articulation) as the vowel it replaces: i/ī and y are both tālavya, u/ū and v are both oṣṭhya, etc.

---

## 2. Yantāvantādeśa Sandhi (यण्तावन्तादेश सन्धि) -- Yan Sandhi with Dīrgha

### Rule

This is a special extension of Yan Sandhi. When the preceding vowel (i/ī, u/ū, ṛ) is preceded by a conjunct consonant cluster, the semivowel replacement still occurs, and in some cases the following vowel takes a dīrgha (long) form.

### Sūtra

> This is governed by additional vārttikas on Yan sandhi.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| stri + ai | stryai | ī + ai → y + ai |
| dhātrī + ādeśaḥ | dhātryādeśaḥ | ī + ā → y + ā (ā remains dīrgha) |

### Key Points

- This is essentially the same mechanism as Yan Sandhi but noted separately because the phonetic environment (conjuncts) makes the resulting pronunciation distinct.

---

## 3. Savarṇadīrgha Sandhi (सवर्णदीर्घ सन्धि)

### Rule

When two **similar** (savarṇa) vowels come together, they are replaced by the **long** (dīrgha) form of that vowel.

### Sūtra

> akaḥ savarṇe dīrghaḥ (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः) -- Pāṇini 6.1.101

### Table

| Pūrva | Para | Result |
|-------|------|--------|
| a / ā | a / ā | ā |
| i / ī | i / ī | ī |
| u / ū | u / ū | ū |
| ṛ / ṝ | ṛ / ṝ | ṝ |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| vidyā + ālayaḥ | vidyālayaḥ | ā + ā → ā |
| kavi + indraḥ | kavīndraḥ | i + i → ī |
| guru + upadeśaḥ | gurūpadeśaḥ | u + u → ū |
| mahā + ātmā | mahātmā | ā + ā → ā |
| giri + īśaḥ | girīśaḥ | i + ī → ī |

### Key Points

- "Savarṇa" means "of the same class" -- a/ā are savarṇa to each other, i/ī to each other, etc.
- The result is always the dīrgha (long/two-mātrā) form.
- This is also called "dīrgha sandhi" in some textbooks.

---

## 4. Guṇa Sandhi (गुण सन्धि)

### Rule

When **a/ā** is followed by **i/ī, u/ū, or ṛ/ṝ**, the two vowels are replaced by the guṇa vowel: **e, o, or ar** respectively.

### Sūtra

> ādguṇaḥ (आद्गुणः) -- Pāṇini 6.1.87

### Table

| Pūrva | Para | Guṇa Result |
|-------|------|-------------|
| a / ā | i / ī | e |
| a / ā | u / ū | o |
| a / ā | ṛ / ṝ | ar |
| a / ā | ḷ | al |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| nara + indraḥ | narendraḥ | a + i → e |
| mahā + utsavaḥ | mahotsavaḥ | ā + u → o |
| mahā + ṛṣiḥ | maharṣiḥ | ā + ṛ → ar |
| parama + īśvaraḥ | parameśvaraḥ | a + ī → e |
| sūrya + udayaḥ | sūryodayaḥ | a + u → o |

### Key Points

- The ādeśa replaces both the pūrva and para vowels.
- Guṇa vowels are: e (for i-class), o (for u-class), ar (for ṛ-class), al (for ḷ).
- The sūtra "ādguṇaḥ" means "after a/ā, guṇa comes."

---

## 5. Vṛddhi Sandhi (वृद्धि सन्धि)

### Rule

When **a/ā** is followed by **e/ai** or **o/au**, the two vowels are replaced by the vṛddhi vowel: **ai** or **au** respectively.

### Sūtra

> vṛddhireci (वृद्धिरेचि) -- Pāṇini 6.1.88

### Table

| Pūrva | Para | Vṛddhi Result |
|-------|------|---------------|
| a / ā | e / ai | ai |
| a / ā | o / au | au |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| sadā + eva | sadaiva | ā + e → ai |
| mahā + aiśvaryam | mahaiśvaryam | ā + ai → ai |
| mahā + oṣadhiḥ | mahauṣadhiḥ | ā + o → au |
| jala + oghaḥ | jalaughaḥ | a + o → au |
| tava + aikyam | tavaikyam | a + ai → ai |

### Key Points

- Vṛddhi vowels are ai and au.
- This sandhi is an extension of Guṇa Sandhi: where Guṇa handles i/u/ṛ class, Vṛddhi handles e/o class (which are themselves guṇa grades).

---

## 5b. Ayavāyāva Sandhi (अयवायाव सन्धि)

### Rule

When **e, o, ai, or au** stands at the end of a pada and is followed by **any vowel**, each is replaced by its corresponding semivowel form:

| Pūrva | Result before a vowel |
|-------|-----------------------|
| e | **ay** |
| o | **av** |
| ai | **āy** |
| au | **āv** |

### Sūtra

> eco'yavāyāvaḥ (एचोऽयवायावः) — Pāṇini 6.1.78

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| hare + i (viz. hare + ye → hareye) | hareye | e + vowel → ay + vowel |
| viṣṇo + e (viṣṇave) | viṣṇave | o + vowel → av + vowel |
| nai + aka | nāyaka | ai + vowel → āy + vowel |
| nau + ika | nāvika | au + vowel → āv + vowel |
| bho + ati (bhavati) | bhavati | o + vowel → av + vowel (inside the verb form) |

### Key Points

- Ayavāyāva is what happens to e / o / ai / au when Pūrvarūpa **does not** apply (i.e., when the following vowel is not a short a at pada-anta).
- The e/o family lands on the -ay/-av semivowel; the vṛddhi ai/au land on the long-vowelled -āy/-āv.
- These are the same eco letters (e, o, ai, au) that also drive Vṛddhi Sandhi as *para* — here they are the pūrva.

---

## 5c. Lopaḥ Śākalyasya (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)

### Rule

When **y** or **v** stands at the end of a pada, preceded by a/ā and followed by a vowel or a soft consonant, the y or v may be **optionally elided** (lopa).

### Sūtra

> lopaḥ śākalyasya (लोपः शाकल्यस्य) — Pāṇini 8.3.19

### Conditions

- Pūrva: pada-final **y** or **v**, immediately preceded by **a** or **ā**.
- Para: any vowel or any voiced (soft) consonant.
- The elision is optional — Śākalya permits it; other schools may keep the y/v.

### Examples

| Before (after Ayavāyāva) | After (Śākalya's lopa) | Explanation |
|--------------------------|------------------------|-------------|
| harayaḥ + iha → haray iha | **haraya iha** | y after a is elided |
| viṣṇavaḥ + iha → viṣṇav iha | **viṣṇava iha** | v after a is elided |
| tay + iha (from te + iha) | **ta iha** | y after a is elided |

### Key Points

- Śākalya's lopa is a common consequence of Ayavāyāva: first e → ay before a vowel, then optionally the y drops, leaving just the a.
- The rule bears an ācārya's name — one of the very few sandhi rules in Pāṇini's system named after the grammarian who preserved it (Śākalya).
- Because it is optional, both forms are grammatical.

---

## 6. Pūrvarūpa Sandhi (पूर्वरूप सन्धि)

### Rule

When **e** or **o** at the end of a **pada** (complete word) is followed by **a**, the **a** is deleted, and the pūrva (preceding) vowel's form remains. An avagraha (ऽ) symbol marks the elided **a** in script.

### Sūtra

> eṅaḥ padāntād ati (एङः पदान्तादति) -- Pāṇini 6.1.109

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| hare + atra | hare'tra | a after e is deleted |
| vane + asmin | vane'smin | a after e is deleted |
| viṣṇo + arpitam | viṣṇo'rpitam | a after o is deleted |
| prabho + anugraham | prabho'nugrahm | a after o is deleted |

### Key Points

- The avagraha symbol (ऽ) indicates where the **a** was elided.
- This applies only when the pūrva is at the end of a complete pada (not inside a word).
- The transformation is a **lopa** (deletion) of the para vowel (a-kāra).
- Pūrvarūpa means "the form of the preceding letter" prevails.

---

## 7. Pararūpa Sandhi (पररूप सन्धि)

### Rule

When an **upasarga** (prefix) ending in **a** is followed by a **dhātu** (verb root) beginning with **a** or **ā**, the two merge and the **para** (following) vowel's form prevails.

### Sūtra

> eṅi pararūpam (एणि पररूपम्) -- applied in upasarga+dhātu context

### Conditions

This sandhi applies only when:
1. The pūrva is an upasarga (verbal prefix), AND
2. The dhātu begins with **a** (specifically the roots **aj** or **edh** are the classic cases).

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| upa + ajati | upajati | a + a → a (pararūpa) |
| pra + ajati | prajati | a + a → a (pararūpa) |

### Key Points

- Pararūpa means "the form of the following letter" prevails.
- This is a very restricted sandhi -- it applies only with specific upasarga + dhātu combinations.
- In the high-school syllabus, very few examples are encountered.

# Part II: Hal Sandhi (Vyañjana / Consonant Sandhi)

## 8. Śchutva Sandhi (श्चुत्व सन्धि)

### Rule

When **sakāra** (s) or **tavarga** letters (t, th, d, dh, n) come in contact with **śakāra** (ś) or **cavarga** letters (c, ch, j, jh, ñ), the sakāra becomes **śakāra** and tavarga letters become their corresponding **cavarga** letters.

### Sūtra

> stoḥ ścunā ścuḥ (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः) -- Pāṇini 8.4.40

### Table

| Original | Becomes |
|----------|---------|
| s | ś |
| t | c |
| th | ch |
| d | j |
| dh | jh |
| n | ñ |

The transformation happens in **both directions** -- the sthānin (letter being replaced) can be on the left side or the right side.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| rāmas + śete | rāmaś śete | s → ś before ś |
| sat + cit | sac cit | t → c before c |
| tat + śrutvā | tac chrutvā | t → c before ś |
| haris + śiśuḥ | hariś śiśuḥ | s → ś before ś |
| ud + cinoti | uc cinoti | d → c before c |

### Key Points

- The sthānin can be in pūrva or para position.
- The key recognition: whenever s/tavarga meets ś/cavarga, transformation to ś/cavarga occurs.

---

## 9. Ṣṭutva Sandhi (ष्टुत्व सन्धि)

### Rule

When **sakāra** (s) or **tavarga** letters (t, th, d, dh, n) come in contact with **ṣakāra** (ṣ) or **ṭavarga** letters (ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ), the sakāra becomes **ṣakāra** and tavarga letters become their corresponding **ṭavarga** letters.

### Sūtra

> ṣṭunā ṣṭuḥ (ष्टुना ष्टुः) -- Pāṇini 8.4.41

### Table

| Original | Becomes |
|----------|---------|
| s | ṣ |
| t | ṭ |
| th | ṭh |
| d | ḍ |
| dh | ḍh |
| n | ṇ |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| rāmas + ṭīkā | rāmaṣ ṭīkā | s → ṣ before ṭ |
| tat + ṭīkā | taṭ ṭīkā | t → ṭ before ṭ |
| iṣ + taḥ | iṣ ṭaḥ | t → ṭ after ṣ |
| dviṣ + taḥ | dviṣ ṭaḥ | t → ṭ after ṣ |

### Key Points

- Like Śchutva, the transformation is bidirectional.
- This sandhi is called "ṣṭutva" because ṣ and ṭ class letters are the result.

---

## 10. Jaśtva Sandhi (जश्त्व सन्धि)

### Rule

When the **first letter** (prathama) of any varga (k, c, ṭ, t, p) is at the end of a word and is followed by a **voiced** sound (ghoṣa vyañjana, i.e., 3rd/4th letters, nasals, semivowels, or vowels), it is replaced by the **third letter** (tṛtīya) of its own varga.

### Sūtra

> jhalaṃ jaśo'nte (झलां जशोऽन्ते) -- Pāṇini 8.2.39; jaśtva via 8.4.53

### Table

| Prathama (1st) | Ādeśa (3rd) |
|---------------|-------------|
| k | g |
| c | j |
| ṭ | ḍ |
| t | d |
| p | b |

### Condition

The para (following letter) must be a ghoṣa sound: any vowel, 3rd/4th/5th letters of any varga, or y/v/r/l.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| vāk + īśaḥ | vāg īśaḥ | k → g before vowel |
| tat + eva | tad eva | t → d before vowel |
| jagat + nāthaḥ | jagad nāthaḥ | t → d before n |
| samrāṭ + jayati | samrāḍ jayati | ṭ → ḍ before j |
| sup + antam | sub antam | p → b before vowel |

### Key Points

- The first letter becoming the third letter is the defining characteristic of Jaśtva.
- This often occurs as an intermediate step before Anunāsika Sandhi applies further.
- Example: jagat + nāṭyam → jagad nāṭyam (jaśtva) → jagan nāṭyam (anunāsika).

---

## 11. Anunāsika Sandhi (अनुनासिक सन्धि)

### Rule

When a vyañjana at the end of a word (excluding the 5th/nasal letter) is followed by a **nasal letter** (any of ṅ, ñ, ṇ, n, m), it is **optionally** replaced by the **fifth letter** (pañcama / anunāsika) of its own varga.

### Sūtra

> Based on Pāṇini 8.4.45

### Table

| Pūrva Varga | Anunāsika Ādeśa |
|-------------|-----------------|
| kavarga (k, kh, g, gh) | ṅ |
| cavarga (c, ch, j, jh) | ñ |
| ṭavarga (ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh) | ṇ |
| tavarga (t, th, d, dh) | n |
| pavarga (p, ph, b, bh) | m |

### Condition

- Pūrva: any vyañjana (class consonant) at end of a word, except the 5th letter itself.
- Para: any anunāsika (nasal) letter.
- The ādeśa takes the anunāsika of the **pūrva's** varga, regardless of which nasal is in the para.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| jagat + nāthaḥ | jagan nāthaḥ | d (from jaśtva) → n (tavarga nasal) |
| vāk + mayam | vāṅ mayam | k → ṅ (kavarga nasal) |
| ṣaṭ + mukhaḥ | ṣaṇ mukhaḥ | ṭ → ṇ (ṭavarga nasal) |
| samrāṭ + nayati | samrāṇ nayati | ṭ → ṇ before n |

### Key Points

- This sandhi is **optional** (vikalpa) when it occurs between two separate words (pada-anta).
- It becomes **mandatory** (nitya) when the para is part of a pratyaya (suffix), e.g., chinmayaḥ from cit + mayaḥ (t → n before m).
- Often Jaśtva applies first, then Anunāsika applies to the result.

---

## 12. Chartva Sandhi (चर्त्व सन्धि)

### Rule

When the **first four letters** of any varga (i.e., the non-nasal stops) stand at the end of a word and are followed by a **kharaḥ** (voiceless consonant: prathama or dvitīya of any varga, or ś, ṣ, s), the final consonant is replaced by the **first letter** (prathama) of its own varga.

### Sūtra

> Based on Pāṇini 8.4.55 (khari ca)

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| samrāḍ + kṛtam | samrāṭ kṛtam | ḍ → ṭ before k (kharaḥ) |
| jagad + phalam | jagat phalam | d → t before ph |
| vāg + ca | vāk ca | g → k before c |

### Key Points

- This is essentially the reverse context of Jaśtva: before voiceless consonants, you get the voiceless (prathama) form.
- Chartva ensures the final consonant matches the voicing of the following sound.

---

## 13. Anusvāra Sandhi (अनुस्वार सन्धि)

### Rule

When **makāra** (m) at the end of a word is followed by any **vyañjana** (consonant), the makāra is replaced by **anusvāra** (ṁ).

### Sūtra

> mo'nusvāraḥ (मोऽनुस्वारः) -- Pāṇini 8.3.23

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| kṛṣṇam + vande | kṛṣṇaṁ vande | m → ṁ before v |
| rāmam + jayati | rāmaṁ jayati | m → ṁ before j |
| gurum + namati | guruṁ namati | m → ṁ before n |
| dharmam + cara | dharmaṁ cara | m → ṁ before c |
| satyam + vada | satyaṁ vada | m → ṁ before v |

### Key Points

- This is the simplest vyañjana sandhi.
- The condition: makāra must be at end of a word, followed by any consonant.
- If followed by a svara (vowel), this sandhi does **not** apply -- the m remains.
- The anusvāra is written as a dot (bindu) above the preceding vowel in Devanāgarī.

---

## 14. Parasavarṇa Sandhi (परसवर्ण सन्धि)

### Rule

This sandhi has three sub-cases:

**Case 1 (Mandatory, padya/internal):** When anusvāra occurs **within** a word and is followed by a varga consonant, it is replaced by the **pañcama** (fifth/nasal) letter of the **following** consonant's varga.

**Case 2 (Optional, pada-anta):** When anusvāra occurs at the **end** of a word and is followed by a varga consonant, it is **optionally** replaced by the pañcama of the following varga.

**Case 3 (Tavarga + Lakāra):** When a tavarga letter is followed by lakāra, the tavarga is replaced by lakāra. If the tavarga letter is nakāra, it becomes the anunāsika (nasal) form of lakāra.

### Sūtra

> anusvārasya yayi parasavarṇaḥ (अनुस्वारस्य ययि परसवर्णः) -- Pāṇini 8.4.58
> torli (तोर्लि) -- Pāṇini 8.4.60

### Table (Cases 1 & 2)

| Anusvāra + Para Varga | Ādeśa |
|-----------------------|-------|
| ṁ + kavarga (k, kh, g, gh) | ṅ |
| ṁ + cavarga (c, ch, j, jh) | ñ |
| ṁ + ṭavarga (ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh) | ṇ |
| ṁ + tavarga (t, th, d, dh) | n |
| ṁ + pavarga (p, ph, b, bh) | m |

### Table (Case 3 -- torli)

| Pūrva | + la | Result |
|-------|------|--------|
| tavarga (t, d, etc.) | l | l (doubled: ll) |
| na | l | anunāsika l (n̐l) |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| nandī (naṁ + dī internally) | nandī | ṁ → n before d (Case 1, mandatory) |
| alaṁ + cakāra | alañ cakāra | ṁ → ñ before c (Case 2, optional) |
| taṁ + daṇḍam | tan daṇḍam | ṁ → n before d |
| bhagavat + līlā | bhagaval līlā | t → l before l (Case 3) |
| gavan + liti | gaval̃ liti | n → nasal l before l (Case 3) |

### Key Points

- "Parasavarṇa" means "a similar letter of the para (following) letter."
- Case 1 (within a word) is always mandatory.
- Case 2 (between words) is optional.
- Case 3 (torli) is a special rule for tavarga letters before lakāra.
- For anunāsika-type ya, va, la: the nasal form (with candrabindu) appears when nakāra precedes them.

# Part III: Viśeṣa Sandhi (Special Sandhis)

These sandhis include visarga transformations and other special rules that do not fall neatly into the svara or vyañjana categories alone.

## 15. Chatva Sandhi (छत्व सन्धि)

### Rule

**Case 1 (Compulsory):** When a short vowel (hrasva svara) ending in itself (not at pada-anta) is followed by **chakāra** (ch), a **takāra** is inserted (āgama) between them, which then becomes chakāra by Śchutva, yielding **cch**.

**Case 2 (Optional):** When a short vowel at the end of a **pada** (word) is followed by **chakāra**, takāra is optionally inserted, yielding the doubled form **cch**.

### Sūtra

> Based on Pāṇini 6.1.73 (che ca) and related rules

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| sva + chanda | svacchanda | t-āgama → cch (compulsory) |
| anu + cheda | anuccheda | t-āgama → cch (compulsory) |
| a + chādanam | ācchādanam | t-āgama → cch |
| vṛkṣa + chāyā | vṛkṣacchāyā | optional doubling |

### Key Points

- The name "chatva" refers to the ch-sound that results.
- The inserted t becomes ch by the action of Śchutva Sandhi.
- When occurring inside a word (prakṛti-pratyaya boundary), it is compulsory.
- When occurring between two separate padas, it is optional.

---

## 16. Pūrvasavarṇa Sandhi (पूर्वसवर्ण सन्धि)

### Rule

When the first four letters (jhal) of any varga are followed by **hakāra** (h), the hakāra is **optionally** replaced by the **fourth letter** (caturthi) of the **preceding** consonant's varga.

### Sūtra

> jharo jhari savarṇe (based on Pāṇini 8.4.65, with hakāra specifics)

### Table

| Pūrva Varga | h → 4th letter |
|-------------|---------------|
| kavarga | gh |
| cavarga | jh |
| ṭavarga | ḍh |
| tavarga | dh |
| pavarga | bh |

### Sub-rule (Case 2)

When the upasargas **ut** (उद्) or **ud** precede the dhātus **sthā** or **stambh**, the sakāra of the dhātu is replaced by **ṭhakāra** (ṭh). This is also classified under Pūrvasavarṇa.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| vāg + harati | vāg gharati | h → gh (kavarga 4th) |
| dig + hīnaḥ | dig ghīnaḥ | h → gh |
| ab + hrasaḥ | ab bhrasaḥ | h → bh (pavarga 4th) |
| etat + hi | etad dhi | h → dh (tavarga 4th) |
| ut + sthātum | uṭṭhātum | s → ṭh (special case) |

### Key Points

- The sandhi is optional in most cases.
- It applies when jhal (non-nasal stops) precede hakāra.
- The fourth letter replaces hakāra because hakāra is mahāprāṇa, and the 4th letter of each varga is also mahāprāṇa.

---

## 17. Numāgama Sandhi (नुमागम सन्धि)

### Rule

When certain letters (nasals: n, ṇ, or ṁ) at the end of a word are preceded by a **hrasva** (short) vowel and followed by any **svara** (vowel), a **num** (nasal consonant, specifically n, ṇ, or the corresponding nasal) is **inserted** (āgama) before the following vowel.

### Sūtra

> Based on various Pāṇini sūtras governing num-āgama

### Conditions

- The pūrva must have a hrasva svara before the final nasal/anusvāra.
- The para must begin with a svara.
- The āgama is the nasal itself being doubled (the num joins before the para svara).

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| praśān + ātmā | praśānnātmā | n-āgama: extra n inserted |
| svamin + ādīnam | svaminnādīnam | n doubled before ā |

### Key Points

- This is an āgama (insertion), not an ādeśa (replacement).
- The inserted nasal coexists with the existing sounds -- both old and new are present.
- The name "numāgama" means "insertion of num (nasal augment)."

---

## 18. Tugāgama Sandhi (तुगागम सन्धि)

### Rule

When a short vowel (hrasva) or visarga-ending word is followed by **chakāra** (ch), a **takāra** is inserted as an āgama; alternatively, when a word ends in a short vowel and is followed by **ś** (śakāra), a **takāra** is optionally inserted. The t then transforms to ch by Śchutva.

**Case 1 (Compulsory):** Short vowel + ch → t is inserted → tch → cch.

**Case 2 (Optional):** Short vowel at pada-end + ś → t is optionally inserted → tś → cch.

### Sūtra

> Based on Pāṇini 6.1.73 and related

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| saṃ + vṛta + chadanam | saṃvṛtacchadanam | t-āgama before ch |
| ā + chādayati | ācchādayati | t-āgama before ch |
| pra + channa | pracchanna | t-āgama → cch |

### Key Points

- Tugāgama and Chatva are closely related -- both involve insertion of t before ch.
- The tug specifically refers to the Pāṇinian technical name for this particular āgama.

---

## 19. Satva Sandhi (सत्व सन्धि)

### Rule

When a word ends in **nakāra** (n) and is followed by certain consonants, the nakāra is replaced by **sakāra** (s), and an anusvāra is added before the sakāra (on top of the preceding vowel). After this, the sakāra may further transform by Śchutva or Ṣṭutva.

### Sūtra

> Based on Pāṇini 8.3.7 (naś chapadāntasya jhali)

### Table

| Original | + Para | Step 1 (satva) | Step 2 (further sandhi) |
|----------|--------|---------------|------------------------|
| n | ch | ṁs + ch | ṁś ch (by śchutva) |
| n | ṭ | ṁs + ṭ | ṁṣ ṭ (by ṣṭutva) |
| n | t | ṁs + t | ṁs t (remains) |
| n | ś | ṁs + ś | ṁś ś (by śchutva) |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| asmin + ca | asmiṁś ca | n → ṁs, then s → ś before c |
| tān + ca | tāṁś ca | n → s with anusvāra, s → ś |
| tān + ṭīkā | tāṁṣ ṭīkā | n → s, s → ṣ before ṭ |
| mahān + tapasvī | mahāṁs tapasvī | n → s with anusvāra |

### Key Points

- Satva = "becoming sa" -- the nakāra transforms to sakāra.
- The anusvāra preserves the nasal quality of the original n.
- After satva occurs, Śchutva or Ṣṭutva may apply to the resulting sakāra.
- In Devanāgarī, the anusvāra appears as a dot (or candrabindu for anunāsika pronunciation).

---

## 20. Lopa Sandhi (लोप सन्धि)

### Rule

In certain contexts, a sound is deleted entirely. The most common lopa in visarga sandhi is when visarga disappears:

1. When visarga preceded by **a** is followed by a **voiced consonant** (3rd/4th letters, nasals, semivowels) or a vowel other than a, the visarga undergoes lopa (deletion) and the a may lengthen.
2. Terminal consonants may undergo lopa in specific environments.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| rāmaḥ + gacchati | rāmo gacchati | (visarga → o before voiced) |
| devāḥ + vadanti | devā vadanti | āḥ → ā before voiced consonant (lopa of visarga) |
| bhavataḥ + dṛṣṭam | bhavato dṛṣṭam | aḥ → o before voiced |

### Key Points

- Lopa means complete disappearance of a sound.
- The visarga lopa cases are systematically handled in the visarga sandhi rules below.

---

## 21. Ukārādeśa Sandhi (उकारादेश सन्धि / Visarga → u)

### Rule

When visarga is preceded by **a** and followed by a **voiced consonant** (3rd, 4th, 5th letters, semivowels, or h), the visarga and the preceding **a** together become **o**.

### Sūtra

> hashi ca (हशि च) -- Pāṇini 6.1.114 (with ato roraplutādaplute)

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| rāmaḥ + hasati | rāmo hasati | aḥ + h → o |
| bālaḥ + vadati | bālo vadati | aḥ + v → o |
| devaḥ + jayati | devo jayati | aḥ + j → o |
| naraḥ + gacchati | naro gacchati | aḥ + g → o |
| eṣaḥ + gaṇeśaḥ | eṣo gaṇeśaḥ | aḥ + g → o |

### Key Points

- The combination aḥ → o is one of the most frequently encountered visarga sandhis.
- When followed by **a** specifically, the result is o + avagraha (pūrvarūpa applies): rāmaḥ + atra → rāmo'tra.

---

## 22. Rephādeśa Sandhi (रेफादेश सन्धि / Visarga → r)

### Rule

When visarga is preceded by a vowel **other than a or ā** (i.e., any of i, ī, u, ū, e, ai, o, au, ṛ) and is followed by a **voiced sound** (voiced consonant or vowel), the visarga is replaced by **repha** (r).

### Sūtra

> sasajuṣo ruḥ (ससजुषो रुः) -- Pāṇini 8.2.66 (visarga to ru); followed by context rules

### Sub-cases

**Case 1:** Visarga preceded by any vowel except a/ā, followed by a voiced consonant or vowel → visarga becomes **r**.

**Case 2:** Visarga preceded by **ā** at the end of an *avyaya* (indeclinable), followed by a voiced sound → visarga becomes **r**. (An avyaya is a word that does not change form: ca, punaḥ, antaḥ, etc.)

**Case 3:** Visarga from a rephānta-śabda (a word whose stem ends in r) in the sambodhana (vocative) form, followed by a voiced sound → visarga becomes **r**.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| muniḥ + āgataḥ | munir āgataḥ | iḥ + ā → r (Case 1) |
| guruḥ + uvāca | gurur uvāca | uḥ + u → r (Case 1) |
| punaḥ + api | punar api | aḥ (avyaya) + a → r (Case 2) |
| aśīḥ + yuddham | aśīr yuddham | īḥ + y → r (Case 1) |
| mātuḥ + āśīḥ | mātur āśīḥ | uḥ + ā → r (Case 1) |

### Key Points

- "Repha" literally means the r-sound.
- The three sub-cases distinguish based on what vowel precedes the visarga and whether the word is an avyaya or rephānta noun.
- After rephādeśa, further svara sandhi (like guṇa) may apply.

---

## 23. Sakārādeśa Sandhi (सकारादेश सन्धि / Visarga → s)

### Rule

When visarga is followed by a **kharaḥ vyañjana** (voiceless consonant) belonging to **tavarga** (t, th) or **cavarga** (c, ch) or **ṭavarga** (ṭ, ṭh) or the sibilants ś and ṣ, the visarga is replaced by **sakāra** (s). After this sakāra appears, further Śchutva or Ṣṭutva may apply.

### Sūtra

> visarjanīyasya saḥ (विसर्जनीयस्य सः) -- Pāṇini 8.3.34 (in relevant contexts)

### Table

| Visarga + Para | Step 1 | Step 2 (further) | Final |
|---------------|--------|-------------------|-------|
| ḥ + t/th | s + t/th | -- | st/sth |
| ḥ + c/ch | s + c/ch | s → ś (śchutva) | śc/śch |
| ḥ + ṭ/ṭh | s + ṭ/ṭh | s → ṣ (ṣṭutva) | ṣṭ/ṣṭh |
| ḥ + ś | s + ś | s → ś (śchutva) | śś (or ḥ remains) |
| ḥ + ṣ | s + ṣ | s → ṣ (ṣṭutva) | ṣṣ (or ḥ remains) |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| rāmaḥ + tatra | rāmas tatra | ḥ → s before t |
| hariḥ + sādhayati | haris sādhayati | ḥ → s before s |
| rāmaḥ + ca | rāmaś ca | ḥ → s → ś before c |
| namaḥ + te | namas te | ḥ → s before t |
| dhanuḥ + ṭaṅkāram | dhanuṣ ṭaṅkāram | ḥ → s → ṣ before ṭ |

### Key Points

- This is the most mechanical visarga sandhi: visarga simply becomes s, then further consonant sandhis may apply.
- When followed by ś or ṣ, there are two possible outcomes: sakāra (which transforms), or visarga remains unchanged (the optional variant).
- The rule excludes ka, kha, pa, pha -- those are handled by Jihvāmūlīya/Upadhmānīya.

---

## 24. Jihvāmūlīya and Upadhmānīya Sandhi (जिह्वामूलीय / उपध्मानीय सन्धि)

### Rule

When visarga is followed by **ka/kha** (kavarga kharaḥ), it optionally becomes **jihvāmūlīya** (a sound produced at the root of the tongue, written with a special symbol). When visarga is followed by **pa/pha** (pavarga kharaḥ), it optionally becomes **upadhmānīya** (a labial fricative, also written with a special symbol).

Alternatively, the visarga simply remains as visarga.

### Sūtra

> Based on descriptions in Prātiśākhyas and Śikṣā texts

### Examples

| Before | After (with special sounds) | After (simplified) |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| rāmaḥ + karoti | rāmaḫ karoti (jihvāmūlīya) | rāmaḥ karoti |
| rāmaḥ + pūjayati | rāmaᳶ pūjayati (upadhmānīya) | rāmaḥ pūjayati |
| viṣṇuḥ + kr̥pāluḥ | viṣṇuḫ kr̥pāluḥ | viṣṇuḥ kr̥pāluḥ |

### Key Points

- In modern usage, visarga before ka/kha and pa/pha is typically left as visarga itself.
- The jihvāmūlīya and upadhmānīya sounds are described in Śikṣā texts and Prātiśākhyas but are rarely written in standard Devanāgarī today.
- These are **optional** transformations.

---

## 25. Rephaādeśa Sandhi in Visarga (विसर्गस्य पूर्वरूप / Visarga Pūrvarūpa)

### Rule

When visarga is preceded by **a** and followed by **a** (the specific case of aḥ + a), the result is **o** and the following **a** is elided, marked by avagraha.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| rāmaḥ + atra | rāmo'tra | aḥ + a → o + avagraha |
| śivaḥ + arjunam | śivo'rjunam | aḥ + a → o + avagraha |
| devaḥ + asti | devo'sti | aḥ + a → o + avagraha |

### Key Points

- This combines Ukārādeśa (aḥ → o before voiced sounds) with Pūrvarūpa (deletion of following a).
- The avagraha marks the deleted a.

---

## 26. Visarga Lopa (āḥ before voiced sounds) (विसर्गलोप)

### Rule

When visarga is preceded by **ā** and followed by a **voiced consonant** (3rd/4th/5th letters, semivowels, h) or any vowel **other than a**, the visarga undergoes **lopa** (deletion), and ā remains.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| devāḥ + vadanti | devā vadanti | āḥ → ā (visarga deleted) |
| bālakāḥ + gacchanti | bālakā gacchanti | āḥ → ā |
| narāḥ + hasanti | narā hasanti | āḥ → ā |

### Key Points

- When āḥ is followed by a soft/voiced consonant, the visarga simply drops.
- This is distinct from the aḥ case (which gives o).

---

## 27. Visarga Rakārādeśa (Rephaādeśa in specialized rephānta words)

### Rule

This combines rephādeśa with subsequent svara sandhis. When a rephānta word (stem ending in r) has visarga in the sambodhana or prāthamika form, and it is followed by a vowel, the visarga becomes r, and then guṇa or other svara sandhi may apply.

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| punaḥ + uktiḥ | punaruktih | ḥ → r, then no further svara sandhi |
| punaḥ + āgamanam | punarāgamanam | ḥ → r before ā |
| antaḥ + āsanam | antarāsanam | ḥ → r before ā |

### Key Points

- The rephādeśa often triggers a chain: visarga → r → then svara sandhi with the following vowel.
- This is particularly common with avyayas like punaḥ, antaḥ, prātaḥ.

# Part IV: Word-Final Consonant Rules (Pada-Anta)

These rules govern what a consonant becomes at the end of a **pada** (fully-formed word) before anything else joins on. They fire before any sandhi across the boundary and often set up the input for the sandhis in Parts II–III.

## 28. Na-Lopa (नकारलोप — Pāṇini 8.2.7)

### Rule

The letter **n** at the end of a **prātipadika** (nominal base) or a **pada** is elided.

### Sūtra

> naḥ (नः) — Pāṇini 8.2.7 (in the chapter *padasya* — "of a pada")

### Examples

| Before (base) | Pada-Anta Form | Explanation |
|---------------|----------------|-------------|
| rājan (n-stem) | rājā | terminal n dropped in the nom.sg |
| ātman | ātmā | terminal n dropped |
| yuvan | yuvā | terminal n dropped |
| brahman | brahmā | terminal n dropped |

### Key Points

- This is the rule that explains why every -an stem nominal (ātman, rājan, brahman, yuvan) surfaces without its n in the nominative singular.
- It is the input for many downstream sandhis: once the n is gone, the preceding a can further behave as a plain vowel-ending word.
- Distinguish from Anunāsika Sandhi (rule 11), which is about a *following* nasal, not about deleting a terminal one.

---

## 29. Ku-Tvam (कुत्व — Pāṇini 8.2.30)

### Rule

A **cavarga** (palatal) consonant at the end of a pada (or before certain consonantal suffixes) is replaced by the **corresponding kavarga** (guttural) letter.

### Sūtra

> coḥ kuḥ (चोः कुः) — Pāṇini 8.2.30 (name of the operation: *ku-tvam*, "becoming a kavarga letter")

### Table

| Original (cavarga) | Ādeśa (kavarga) |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| c | k |
| ch | kh |
| j | g |
| jh | gh |
| ñ | ṅ |

### Examples

| Before | After | Explanation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| vāc (nom.sg) | vāk | c → k at pada-anta (then further sandhi fires: e.g. vāg īśaḥ by Jaśtva) |
| pratyañc + suffix beginning with a consonant | pratyaṅ- | ñ → ṅ |
| samyañc | samyaṅ | ñ → ṅ |
| sṛj (root, in pada-final position) | sṛk (then jaśtva → sṛg before a voiced sound) | j → k |

### Key Points

- Ku-tvam is why words that "look like they end in c or j" in the lexicon (vāc, sṛj) surface as -k, -g at the end of an actual pada.
- The rule fires *before* Jaśtva, Chartva, or Anunāsika — it feeds them.
- Along with Na-lopa, this is the class of rules that tell you *what a word actually ends in* before any sandhi across the boundary can begin.

*Note: e/ai and o/au in pūrva position outside pada-anta contexts follow special rules not fully covered at the high-school level.*

### Hal Sandhi Summary

1. **Śchutva**: s/tavarga + ś/cavarga → ś/cavarga
2. **Ṣṭutva**: s/tavarga + ṣ/ṭavarga → ṣ/ṭavarga
3. **Jaśtva**: 1st letter at pada-end + voiced → 3rd letter
4. **Anunāsika**: consonant + nasal → 5th letter of pūrva's varga (optional)
5. **Chartva**: non-nasal stop + voiceless consonant → 1st letter of own varga
6. **Anusvāra**: m + consonant → anusvāra (ṁ)
7. **Parasavarṇa**: anusvāra + varga consonant → 5th letter of para's varga

### Visarga Sandhi Summary

- aḥ + voiced consonant → **o** (ukārādeśa)
- aḥ + a → **o'** (ukārādeśa + pūrvarūpa)
- āḥ + voiced consonant → **ā** (visarga lopa)
- (i/ī/u/ū)ḥ + voiced → **r** (rephādeśa)
- ḥ + kharaḥ (t,th,c,ch,ṭ,ṭh,ś,ṣ,s) → **s** (sakārādeśa, then further śchutva/ṣṭutva)
- ḥ + ka/kha → **jihvāmūlīya** (optional) or remains ḥ
- ḥ + pa/pha → **upadhmānīya** (optional) or remains ḥ

Now snap them together with sandhi: "namaḥ + śivāya" → "namaś śivāya" (visarga → ś before ś). Try "mahā + īśvara" → "maheśvara" (ā+ī → e, vṛddhi). Every mantra you know is bricks + snap-rules.
