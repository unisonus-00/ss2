# Chapter 1: Varṇavicāraḥ — Letters

## Goal
Understand the complete Sanskrit alphabet (varṇamālā) — its classification, pronunciation, origin, and the Māheśvara Sūtras that encode it for grammar.

---

## 1. Varṇa and Akṣara

A **varṇa** (वर्ण) is a *svatantra dhvani* — an independent, indivisible unit of sound. It is the smallest part of language that cannot be broken further: a, ka, cha — none of these can be split into smaller components.

An **akṣara** (अक्षर) literally means "that which does not perish" (na kṣarati iti akṣaram). Akṣaras combine to form meaningful words; through meaning, they pervade the universe of language. Sarasvatī's akṣamālā (garland of beads) represents this varṇamālā.

**Key point:** Varṇa and akṣara are used interchangeably in vyākaraṇa to mean "letter." The distinction matters only in specific technical contexts (e.g., pūrṇākṣara vs. half-letters).

---

## 2. Varṇavibhāga — Classification of Letters

The letters of Sanskrit are classified into four broad categories:

| Category | Sanskrit | Description | Count |
|----------|----------|-------------|-------|
| Vowels | svarāḥ | Independent sounds — can be pronounced alone | 13 (+ 3 pluta) |
| Consonants | vyañjanāni | Dependent sounds — need a svara to be pronounced | 33 |
| Ayogavāhāḥ | अयोगवाहाः | Neither svara nor vyañjana; seen in usage | 4 |
| Yamāḥ | यमाः | Extra sounds found in Vedic recitation | 8 |

Total letters as per Śikṣā Śāstra (Pāṇinīya Śikṣā): **63**

---

## 3. Svarāḥ — Vowels

**Definition:** *svayaṃ rājante iti svarāḥ* — those which shine (are pronounced) on their own. A svara can be pronounced independently without the help of any other letter.

### 3.1 Classification by Mātrā (duration)

The time taken to pronounce a letter is called a **mātrā**. One mātrā approximates the time of a single involuntary blink of the eye (~1 second).

| Type | Sanskrit | Mātrā Count | Duration | Letters |
|------|----------|-------------|----------|---------|
| Short | hrasva | 1 | ~1 sec | a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ |
| Long | dīrgha | 2 | ~2 sec | ā, ī, ū, ṝ |
| Protracted | pluta | 3 | ~3 sec | a3, i3, u3 (Vedic) |

The verse to remember:

> *ekamātrā bhaved hrasvā, dvimātrā dīrgha ucyate |*
> *trimātrā pluta vijñeyā, vyañjanārdhamātrākam ||*

"Hrasva has one mātrā, dīrgha has two, pluta has three, and a vyañjana has half a mātrā."

**Note on ḷ (ḻkāra):** The Śikṣā Śāstras state that ḷ has no dīrgha form — the dīrgha ḹ is not attested even across the vast Vedic literature.

### 3.2 Samānākṣara and Sandhyakṣara

Svaras are further classified by composition:

| Type | Meaning | Letters |
|------|---------|---------|
| Samānākṣara (simple vowels) | Pure, single-origin vowels | a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, ṛ, ṝ, ḷ |
| Sandhyakṣara (compound vowels) | Formed by combining two samānākṣaras | e, ai, o, au |

The sandhyakṣaras arise as follows:

| Sandhyakṣara | Components | Mātrā |
|--------------|------------|-------|
| e | a + i | 2 (dīrgha) |
| ai | ā + i | 2 (dīrgha) |
| o | a + u | 2 (dīrgha) |
| au | ā + u | 2 (dīrgha) |

Since e = a (1 mātrā) + i (1 mātrā), the resulting e is 2 mātrās — therefore e, ai, o, au are always dīrgha.

### 3.3 Savarṇa — Similar Sounds

Two sounds are **savarṇa** (सवर्ण, "of the same class") when they share both:

1. the same sthāna (place of articulation), *and*
2. the same ābhyantara-prayatna (internal effort).

Savarṇa vowel pairs:

| Pair | Sthāna | Prayatna |
|------|--------|----------|
| a ↔ ā | Kaṇṭha | Vivṛta (saṃvṛta for short a in-word) |
| i ↔ ī | Tālu | Vivṛta |
| u ↔ ū | Oṣṭha | Vivṛta |
| ṛ ↔ ṝ | Mūrdhan | Vivṛta |
| ḷ ↔ (ḹ) | Danta | Vivṛta |

**Savarṇa is the trigger for Savarṇa-dīrgha sandhi** (Pāṇini 6.1.101, *akaḥ savarṇe dīrghaḥ*): when two savarṇa vowels meet, they merge into the dīrgha form. This is why the concept belongs here, in the letters chapter — the sandhi rule in Stage 3 assumes you already know what savarṇa means.

### 3.4 Vowel Accents — Udātta, Anudātta, Svarita

Every Vedic vowel carries one of three pitch-accents:

| Accent | Sanskrit | Pitch |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Udātta | उदात्त — "raised" | Higher pitch |
| Anudātta | अनुदात्त — "not-raised" | Lower pitch |
| Svarita | स्वरित — "sounded / mixed" | A fall from udātta down through anudātta within one syllable |

In classical (laukika) Sanskrit the accent is not written and often not observed, but it is essential for Vedic recitation. In the Kṛṣṇa-Yajurveda tradition the accents are learned by ear, marked in the Devanāgarī script with underscores (anudātta) and vertical strokes (svarita), and taught systematically in **Stage 19 — Svara-Vidyā**. Everything you learn about accent here is a preview of that stage.

### 3.5 Guṇa and Vṛddhi as Vowel Grades

Independently of sandhi, Pāṇini classifies the vowels into three strength-grades:

| Grade | Sanskrit | Vowels | Sūtra |
|-------|----------|--------|-------|
| Base | — | i, ī, u, ū, ṛ, ṝ, ḷ | — |
| Guṇa | गुण — "quality / middle grade" | a, e, o | *adeṅ guṇaḥ* (1.1.2) |
| Vṛddhi | वृद्धि — "strengthening / highest grade" | ā, ai, au | *vṛddhirādaic* (1.1.1) |

Each base vowel has a guṇa form and a vṛddhi form:

| Base | Guṇa | Vṛddhi |
|------|------|--------|
| a / ā | a | ā |
| i / ī | e | ai |
| u / ū | o | au |
| ṛ / ṝ | ar | ār |
| ḷ | al | āl |

This grading powers both Guṇa-sandhi (a/ā + i/ī → e, etc.) and countless verbal-root strengthenings (√kṛ → kar → kār) that you will meet in Stage 5 (Kriyā) and Stage 8 (Dhātu).

## 4. Vyañjanāni — Consonants

**Definition:** *svara-dharmam anugacchanti iti vyañjanāni* — those which follow (depend on) the quality of a svara. A vyañjana cannot be pronounced independently; it needs a svara attached (e.g., ka = k + a).

### 4.1 Sparśa Vyañjanāni — Stop Consonants (25)

Organized by place of articulation (sthāna) in five vargas of five letters each:

| Varga | Sthāna | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th (Anunāsika) |
|-------|--------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----------------|
| ka-varga | Kaṇṭha (throat) | ka | kha | ga | gha | ṅa |
| ca-varga | Tālu (palate) | ca | cha | ja | jha | ña |
| ṭa-varga | Mūrdhan (roof) | ṭa | ṭha | ḍa | ḍha | ṇa |
| ta-varga | Danta (teeth) | ta | tha | da | dha | na |
| pa-varga | Oṣṭha (lips) | pa | pha | ba | bha | ma |

### 4.2 Antaḥstha Vyañjanāni — Semivowels (4)

| Letter | Sthāna |
|--------|--------|
| ya | Tālu (palatal) |
| ra | Mūrdhan (retroflex) |
| la | Danta (dental) |
| va | Dantoṣṭha (labio-dental) |

### 4.3 Ūṣman Vyañjanāni — Sibilants and Aspirate (4)

| Letter | Sthāna |
|--------|--------|
| śa | Tālu (palatal) |
| ṣa | Mūrdhan (retroflex) |
| sa | Danta (dental) |
| ha | Kaṇṭha (throat) |

**Extra Vedic consonant:** ḻa (a variant of ḍa) is found in Vedic texts and some regional languages.

**Total vyañjanāni:** 33 (25 sparśa + 4 antaḥstha + 4 ūṣman). With the Vedic ḻa: 34.

### 4.3 Classification by Mṛdu / Kaṭhina and Prāṇa

| Property | Letters | Count |
|----------|---------|-------|
| **Mṛdvāni** (soft) | 3rd, 4th, 5th of each varga + antaḥstha (ya, ra, la, va) | 15 + 4 = 19* |
| **Karkaśāni** (hard) | 1st, 2nd of each varga + ūṣman (śa, ṣa, sa, ha) | 10 + 4 = 14* |
| **Alpaprāṇa** (light breath) | 1st, 3rd, 5th of each varga + antaḥstha | 15 + 4 = 19 |
| **Mahāprāṇa** (heavy breath) | 2nd, 4th of each varga + ūṣman | 10 + 4 = 14 |

*Note: Some classifications count mṛdu/kaṭhina slightly differently. The transcript gives: mṛdu = 3rd, 4th, 5th + ya, ra, la, va = 20; kaṭhina = 1st, 2nd + śa, ṣa, sa, ha = 14.

Summary grid for each position in a varga:

| Position | Prāṇa | Kaṭhina/Mṛdu | Example (ka-varga) |
|----------|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| 1st | Alpaprāṇa | Kaṭhina (hard, voiceless) | ka |
| 2nd | Mahāprāṇa | Kaṭhina (hard, voiceless aspirate) | kha |
| 3rd | Alpaprāṇa | Mṛdu (soft, voiced) | ga |
| 4th | Mahāprāṇa | Mṛdu (soft, voiced aspirate) | gha |
| 5th | Alpaprāṇa | Anunāsika (nasal) | ṅa |

**Note on ha:** Ha is mahāprāṇa but mṛdu (soft). This is a commonly tested distinction.

## 5. Ayogavāhāḥ

These are sounds that do not fit neatly into svaras or vyañjanāni but are essential in usage:

| Letter | Name | Symbol | Description |
|--------|------|--------|-------------|
| ḥ | Visarga | ः | Voiceless breath after a vowel |
| ṃ | Anusvāra | ं | Nasal resonance |
| x̐ | Anunāsika (Candrabindu) | ँ | Nasalized vowel |
| ẖ | Jihvāmūlīya | ᳶ | Variant of visarga before ka/kha |
| x͎ | Upadhmānīya | ᳷ | Variant of visarga before pa/pha |

The jihvāmūlīya and upadhmānīya share the same written symbol (ardhavisarga) but differ in context — jihvāmūlīya occurs before ka-varga, upadhmānīya before pa-varga.

Ayogavāhāḥ are not included in the Māheśvara Sūtras. They are separately classified and behave according to their own rules.

---

## 6. Yamāḥ

Yamāḥ are additional sounds found primarily in Vedic recitation. They arise when the 5th letter (anunāsika) of a varga is followed by any of the first four letters of the same or another varga. There are **8 yamāḥ** — one corresponding to each of the first four letters in the ka-varga and ca-varga groups (and by extension, other vargas).

Example: When ṅ (5th of ka-varga) is followed by ka, a yama sound is produced at the junction.

Yamāḥ are marked with special notation in Vedic texts and are not normally encountered in classical Sanskrit.

---

## 7. Guṇitākṣarāḥ — Vowel-Consonant Combinations

When a vyañjana combines with different svaras, it produces **guṇitākṣaras** (qualified letters):

| Consonant + Svara | Result | Example |
|-------------------|--------|---------|
| k + a | ka | default form |
| k + ā | kā | कां |
| k + i | ki | कि |
| k + ī | kī | की |
| k + u | ku | कु |
| k + ū | kū | कू |
| k + e | ke | के |
| k + ai | kai | कै |
| k + o | ko | को |
| k + au | kau | कौ |

The mātrā notation (the diacritical marks in Devanāgarī) indicates which svara accompanies the consonant. Every consonant in the varṇamālā has this full set of guṇitākṣara forms.

---

## 8. Saṃyuktākṣarāḥ — Conjunct Consonants

When two or more consonants appear together without an intervening vowel, they form a **saṃyuktākṣara** (conjunct):

| Type | Example | Devanāgarī |
|------|---------|------------|
| Vertical stack | kta (k + ta) | क्त |
| Horizontal join | kṣa (k + ṣa) | क्ष |
| Special forms | jña (j + ña) | ज्ञ |
| Three consonants | stra (s + t + ra) | स्त्र |

Common conjuncts to recognize: kṣa (क्ष), jña (ज्ञ), tra (त्र), śra (श्र), dya (द्य), hma (ह्म).

---

## 9. Varṇotpatti — Origin of Letters (Sthāna and Prayatna)

Every letter originates from a specific place in the vocal apparatus (sthāna) and is produced through a specific articulatory effort (prayatna).

### 9.1 Sthāna — Place of Articulation

| Sthāna | Sanskrit | Letters Produced |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| Kaṇṭha (throat) | कण्ठ | a, ā, ka-varga, ha, visarga |
| Tālu (palate) | तालु | i, ī, ca-varga, ya, śa |
| Mūrdhan (roof of mouth) | मूर्धन् | ṛ, ṝ, ṭa-varga, ra, ṣa |
| Danta (teeth) | दन्त | ḷ, ta-varga, la, sa |
| Oṣṭha (lips) | ओष्ठ | u, ū, pa-varga |
| Nāsikā (nose) | नासिका | ña, ṇa, na, ma, ṅa, anusvāra |
| Kaṇṭha-tālu | कण्ठतालु | e, ai |
| Kaṇṭha-oṣṭha | कण्ठोष्ठ | o, au |
| Dantoṣṭha | दन्तोष्ठ | va |

### 9.2 Prayatna — Articulatory Effort

**Ābhyantara prayatna** (internal effort) — what happens inside the mouth:

| Prayatna | Description | Letters |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Spṛṣṭa | Complete contact | sparśa (stop consonants) |
| Īṣat-spṛṣṭa | Slight contact | antaḥstha (semivowels) |
| Īṣad-vivṛta | Slightly open (friction narrow) | ūṣman (sibilants) |
| Vivṛta | Open (no contact) | svaras (vowels) |
| Saṃvṛta | Tightened / contracted | the short **a** in ordinary (laukika) speech |

**Note on saṃvṛta (the short a's private effort):** In isolation the short a is vivṛta like every other vowel. Inside a word the classical Śikṣā tradition says it becomes saṃvṛta — narrower than any other vowel. No other letter shares this effort. This is why the short a is treated as a special case throughout Sanskrit phonetics and grammar (e.g., it is the vowel that most often collapses at the boundary, and the a that guṇa/pararūpa/pūrvarūpa cluster around).

**Bāhya prayatna** (external effort) — voicing and aspiration:

| Prayatna | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Śvāsa | Voiceless (1st, 2nd letters, ūṣman) |
| Nāda | Voiced (3rd, 4th, 5th letters, antaḥstha) |
| Alpaprāṇa | Light aspiration |
| Mahāprāṇa | Heavy aspiration |

The Śikṣā Śāstra verse: *icchā bhavati tadā kaṇṭhādau sthāne prayatnaḥ prāṇavāyuḥ...* — when the desire to speak arises, the prāṇa (breath) is directed to the appropriate sthāna with the appropriate prayatna.

## 10. Māheśvara Sūtrāṇi

### 10.1 Origin

The 14 Māheśvara Sūtras are said to have originated from the ḍamaru (drum) of Lord Śiva at the end of his tāṇḍava dance. Mahāṛṣi Pāṇini received these sounds and used them as the foundation for his Aṣṭādhyāyī (the 3,984 sūtras of Sanskrit grammar).

The verse:
> *nṛttāvasāne naṭarājarājo nanāda ḍhakkāṃ navapañcavāram |*
> *uddhartukāmaḥ sanakādi-siddhān etad vimarśe śivasūtrajālam ||*

"At the end of his dance, Lord Naṭarāja sounded his drum fourteen times, wishing to uplift Sanaka and other siddhas — this is the web of Śiva-sūtras."

### 10.2 The 14 Sūtras

| # | Sūtra | Letters Encoded |
|---|-------|----------------|
| 1 | a i u ṇ | a, i, u |
| 2 | ṛ ḷ k | ṛ, ḷ |
| 3 | e o ṅ | e, o |
| 4 | ai au c | ai, au |
| 5 | ha ya va ra ṭ | ha, ya, va, ra |
| 6 | la ṇ | la |
| 7 | ña ma ṅa ṇa na m | ña, ma, ṅa, ṇa, na |
| 8 | jha bha ñ | jha, bha |
| 9 | gha ḍha dha ṣ | gha, ḍha, dha |
| 10 | ja ba ga ḍa da ś | ja, ba, ga, ḍa, da |
| 11 | kha pha cha ṭha tha ca ṭa ta v | kha, pha, cha, ṭha, tha, ca, ṭa, ta |
| 12 | ka pa y | ka, pa |
| 13 | śa ṣa sa r | śa, ṣa, sa |
| 14 | ha l | ha |

### 10.3 Pratyāhāra — Abbreviatory Notation

A **pratyāhāra** is formed by taking the first letter of any sūtra and combining it with the *it* (final marker letter) of any subsequent sūtra. The pratyāhāra denotes all letters between the starting letter and the *it* letter (the *it* letter itself is excluded).

**Rules:**
1. The *it* (last letter with a stroke/marker) in each sūtra is only a label — it is not counted as a letter of the alphabet.
2. Pratyāhāras can only be formed *within* the sequence of the 14 sūtras — you cannot go backwards.
3. Pratyāhāras cannot be formed between two *it* markers alone.

### 10.4 Key Pratyāhāras

| Pratyāhāra | Start → End | Letters Included | Meaning |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **ac** | a → c (sūtra 4) | a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ, e, o, ai, au | All vowels |
| **hal** | ha → l (sūtra 14) | ha, ya, va, ra, la... all to sa, ha | All consonants |
| **yaṇ** | ya → ṇ (sūtra 6) | ya, va, ra, la | Semivowels (antaḥstha) |
| **aṇ** | a → ṇ (sūtra 1) | a, i, u | The three basic short vowels |
| **ik** | i → k (sūtra 2) | i, u, ṛ, ḷ | Vowels i, u, ṛ, ḷ |
| **uk** | u → k (sūtra 2) | u, ṛ, ḷ | Vowels u, ṛ, ḷ |
| **eṅ** | e → ṅ (sūtra 3) | e, o | Guṇa diphthongs |
| **ec** | e → c (sūtra 4) | e, o, ai, au | All diphthongs |
| **al** | a → l (sūtra 14) | All letters | Universal set (all varṇas) |
| **jhaś** | jha → ś (sūtra 10) | jha, bha, ja, ba, ga, ḍa, da | Voiced non-nasal stops |
| **khar** | kha → r (sūtra 13) | kha, pha, cha, ṭha, tha, ca, ṭa, ta, ka, pa, śa, ṣa, sa | All voiceless consonants |
| **jaś** | ja → ś (sūtra 10) | ja, ba, ga, ḍa, da | 3rd letters of each varga |
| **śal** | śa → l (sūtra 14) | śa, ṣa, sa, ha | Ūṣman letters |

**Where are anusvāra and visarga?** They are ayogavāhāḥ and are not part of the Māheśvara Sūtras. They are treated separately in Pāṇini's grammar.
