# Stage 27: Samasyāpūraṇa — Backwards Composition

## Goal
Given a strange, out-of-context pāda (line), construct the remaining pādas so the whole verse makes sense. This is the foundational avadhāna skill — deriving context creatively from a constraint.

## What is Samasyāpūraṇa?

**Samasya** = problem/puzzle. **Pūraṇa** = completion. You receive one pāda (quarter-verse) — often one that seems bizarre or contradictory in isolation — and must compose the remaining three pādas to create a coherent, meaningful verse.

## The Method

### Step 1: Analyze the given pāda
- What is the literal meaning?
- What meter is it in? (count syllables, determine pattern)
- What are the key words?

### Step 2: Find a context that makes the pāda sensible
- Is there a deity story where this makes sense?
- Can the words be reinterpreted through sandhi-splitting differently?
- Is there a philosophical reading?

### Step 3: Compose the surrounding pādas
- Match the meter
- Build up to the given pāda naturally
- The verse should feel inevitable — the given pāda should seem the perfect conclusion

## Example

**Given pāda (d):** viṣaṃ pītvā sukhī bhavat
("He drank poison and became happy" — seems absurd!)

**Solution:** Create context where this makes sense → Śiva drinking hālāhala!

> devāsura-samudra-manthane purā (a)
> hālāhalaṃ viṣaṃ samutthitam (b)
> jagad-rakṣārthaṃ maheśvaraḥ (c)
> **viṣaṃ pītvā sukhī bhavat** (d)

Now the meaning is clear: "During the churning of the ocean, terrible poison arose. For the world's protection, Maheśvara drank the poison and was content."

## Another Example

**Given pāda:** agnau śītalatā katham
("How can there be coolness in fire?" — paradox!)

**Solution:** Connect to Prahlāda story — Prahlāda survived the fire through Viṣṇu's grace.

> viṣṇu-bhakta-prahlādasya
> hiraṇyakaśipor bhayāt
> agnimadhye sthitasyāpi
> **agnau śītalatā katham?** → because of Viṣṇu's kṛpā!

## Strategy Tips

1. **Mythological context** — most "impossible" pādas become possible in the context of a deity's līlā
2. **Sandhi re-analysis** — break sandhi differently to find new meanings
3. **Metaphorical reading** — "fire" might mean "anger," "drinking poison" might mean "absorbing criticism"
4. **Double meaning (śleṣa)** — words with multiple meanings open new contexts

## Practice Format

You will be given a pāda. Compose the remaining 3 pādas to make a complete anuṣṭubh śloka. Each pāda should be 8 syllables.

## Milestone Check
Can you complete a samasyāpūraṇa — given a strange pāda, construct a coherent verse around it — within 10 minutes?
