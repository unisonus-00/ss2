# Stage 0 Workbook: Devanāgarī — Answers

## A. Read the letter

1. a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ e ai o au
2. ka kha ga gha ṅa
3. ca cha ja jha ña
4. ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa
5. ta tha da dha na
6. pa pha ba bha ma
7. ya ra la va
8. śa ṣa sa ha

Every letter in 2 to 8 is read with a short a attached. क is ka, not k.

## B. The built-in a, and cancelling it

A virāma takes away the a that the letter would otherwise carry. Nothing is
written for that a, so something has to be written to remove it.

1. k
2. nama
3. nam
4. kara
5. tat
6. vad
7. vastram

In 2 and 4 nothing at all is written for the a's. In 3, 5, 6 and 7 the
virāma at the end is the only mark that stops the last letter being read with
one.

## C. The vowel signs

1. kā ki kī ku kū
2. kṛ kṝ kḷ
3. ke kai ko kau
4. gi gī nu nū me mai to tau

## D. Vowel signs that sit oddly

1. The i sign, as in कि. It stands to the left of its consonant and is read
   after it. It is the only sign that does this.
2. hṛ — ह takes the ṛ sign in a place of its own
3. ru
4. rū — र carries u and ū in shapes of their own rather than the ordinary
   signs, and the pair रु / रू is worth learning together
5. kim
6. nadī

## E. Similar characters

1. भ bha, म ma
2. ब ba, व va
3. थ tha, य ya
4. ख kha — one letter; रव rava — two letters, र and व
5. घ gha, ध dha
6. प pa, ष ṣa
7. ङ ṅa, ड ḍa
8. उ u — a vowel letter; ज ja — a consonant

Success on one member of a pair does not mean you can pick out the other, so
both are asked.

## F. Splitting conjuncts

An ordinary conjunct is built by the first consonant giving up its upright
stroke, with the next written against the stump. Where a letter has no such
stroke — द, ट, ठ, ड, ढ, ह — its partner is stacked underneath instead. A
conjunct says one thing only: no vowel comes between these consonants.

1. ṇ + ḍa
2. t + ma
3. t + m + ya
4. k + ka
5. d + ma
6. d + bha
7. d + ya
8. n + na
9. p + ta

Numbers 5, 6 and 7 are the stacked kind; the rest lose a stroke. Number 3
shows the rule applied twice, on three consonants at once.

## G. Conjuncts by sight

1. kṣa — क + ष
2. jña — ज + ञ
3. tta — त + त
4. kta — क + त
5. tra — त + र
6. śva — श + व
7. śca — श + च
8. śra — श + र

These are the ones the rule in F will not get you, which is why they are the
only conjuncts this stage asks you to learn as shapes.

## H. र above the line and below it

1. The hook above ज is a repha: an r sounded before ज. अर्जुनः is arjunaḥ.
2. The stroke under क is a rakāra: an r sounded after क. चक्र is cakra.
3. karma
4. krama
5. dharmaḥ
6. sūryaḥ
7. arghyam
8. karpūraḥ

कर्म and क्रम hold the same three consonants. Only the position of र says
which word it is.

## I. Anusvāra, visarga and punctuation

1. A single dot above the line is the anusvāra: a nasal closing the syllable,
   transliterated ṃ.
2. Two dots after a letter are the visarga: a light breath, transliterated ḥ.
3. haṃsaḥ
4. naraḥ
5. vaṃśaḥ
6. saṃskṛtam
7. । is the daṇḍa — the end of a sentence in prose, or of a line in verse.
8. ॥ is the double daṇḍa, and commonly closes a verse.
