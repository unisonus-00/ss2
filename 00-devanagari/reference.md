## The Consonant Letters

Every one of these is read with a short a attached: क is ka, not k.

### The five rows of stops

| Row | | | | | |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|
| k row | क ka | ख kha | ग ga | घ gha | ङ ṅa |
| c row | च ca | छ cha | ज ja | झ jha | ञ ña |
| ṭ row | ट ṭa | ठ ṭha | ड ḍa | ढ ḍha | ण ṇa |
| t row | त ta | थ tha | द da | ध dha | न na |
| p row | प pa | फ pha | ब ba | भ bha | म ma |

### Semivowels, sibilants and the aspirate

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| य ya | र ra | ल la | व va |
| श śa | ष ṣa | स sa | ह ha |

ङ and ञ are almost never seen standing alone. You will meet them inside
conjuncts — अङ्कुशः, ज्ञानम् — and hardly anywhere else.

## The Vowel Signs (Mātrās)

The same vowel, written as a mark on a consonant rather than as a letter.
Shown here on क.

| Sign on क | IAST | Where it sits |
|-----------|------|---------------|
| क | ka | nothing written; the built-in a |
| का | kā | a bar to the right |
| कि | ki | to the LEFT of the letter, read after it |
| की | kī | to the right, leaning the other way |
| कु | ku | hung below |
| कू | kū | hung below, longer tail |
| कृ | kṛ | hung below as a small hook |
| कॄ | kṝ | hung below; rare |
| कॢ | kḷ | hung below; rarer still |
| के | ke | one stroke above the headline |
| कै | kai | two strokes above |
| को | ko | the ā bar plus one stroke above |
| कौ | kau | the ā bar plus two strokes above |

The i sign is the one place where the order on the page and the order in the
sound come apart. कि is ki. Read the consonant first whatever the eye sees
first.

Three combinations are written where the ordinary rule would not put them,
and are simply conventional.

| Written | Read |
|---------|------|
| हृ | hṛ |
| रु | ru |
| रू | rū |

## Cancelling the Built-in a

| Written | Read | Why |
|---------|------|-----|
| क | ka | nothing said, so the built-in a stands |
| क् | k | the virāma stroke underneath takes the a away |
| क्त | kta | a conjunct: no vowel comes between the two |

A virāma at the end of a word is extremely common, because so many Sanskrit
neuters end in -am: पुष्पम्, त्रिशूलम्, वस्त्रम्.

## Letters That Look Alike

The commonest misreadings, and what actually separates the pair.

Eight sets, and both members of each are worth drilling: being able to pick
out one of a pair does not mean you can pick out the other.

| | | Note |
|---|---|---|
| भ bha | म ma | |
| ब ba | व va | |
| थ tha | य ya | |
| ख kha | रव rava | one letter against two |
| घ gha | ध dha | |
| प pa | ष ṣa | and ष against the other two sibilants, श and स |
| ङ ṅa | ड ḍa | |
| उ u | ज ja | a vowel letter against a consonant |

Exactly how these differ depends on the typeface you are reading. Learn them
as whole shapes in contrast with each other rather than as a list of strokes.

## How an Ordinary Conjunct Is Built

A conjunct says one thing only: no vowel comes between these consonants.

The productive rule is that the first consonant loses its upright stroke and
the next is written against what is left.

| Parts | Written | Read |
|-------|---------|------|
| न + त | न्त | nta |
| स + थ | स्थ | stha |
| ल + प | ल्प | lpa |
| ग + न | ग्न | gna |
| ङ + ग | ङ्ग | ṅga |
| ष + ट | ष्ट | ṣṭa |
| त + व | त्व | tva |
| द + ध | द्ध | ddha |
| ण + ड | ण्ड | ṇḍa |
| त + म | त्म | tma |
| क + क | क्क | kka |
| न + न | न्न | nna |
| प + त | प्त | pta |

Letters with no upright stroke to give up — द, ट, ठ, ड, ढ, ह — stack their
partner underneath instead: द्य dya, द्म dma, द्भ dbha, ह्म hma.

More than two can join, and the rule simply applies twice. त्म्य is
t + m + ya; स्त्र is s + t + ra, and वस्त्रम् is vastram.

## The Two Forms of र

र is the one letter that disappears into a different shape inside a conjunct,
and it does so in two directions.

| Form | Written as | Position | Example |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| repha | a hook riding above a letter | r comes BEFORE that letter | कर्म karma |
| rakāra | a slanted stroke below a letter | r comes AFTER that letter | क्रम krama |

The two examples above hold the same three consonants. Only the position of
र says which word it is.

More: धर्मः dharmaḥ, सूर्यः sūryaḥ, अर्घ्यम् arghyam, कर्पूरः karpūraḥ,
निर्मलम् nirmalam; प्रदक्षिणा pradakṣiṇā, श्री śrī.

## Marks Above, After and Between

| Mark | Name | IAST | What it does |
|------|------|------|--------------|
| ं | anusvāra | ṃ | a nasal closing the syllable — संस्कृतम् saṃskṛtam |
| ः | visarga | ḥ | a light breath after the vowel — नमः namaḥ |
| ँ | candrabindu | m̐ | a nasalised vowel; mostly Vedic |
| ऽ | avagraha | ’ | marks an a that has dropped out — सोऽपि so’pi |
| ् | virāma | — | cancels the built-in a |
| । | daṇḍa | — | end of a line or sentence |
| ॥ | double daṇḍa | — | end of a verse |
| ॐ | oṃ | oṃ | written as one sign of its own |

The visarga closes an enormous number of Sanskrit words, because it is what
the commonest masculine ending looks like: शिवः, गणेशः, कृष्णः.

## Reading a Whole Word

Work left to right, taking one orthographic syllable at a time. Ask of each
one: which consonants, in what order, and what vowel — or no vowel.

| Word | Broken up | Read |
|------|-----------|------|
| कृष्णः | कृ · ष्णः | kṛ-ṣṇaḥ |
| लक्ष्मीः | ल · क्ष्मीः | la-kṣmīḥ |
| अक्षताः | अ · क्ष · ता · ः | a-kṣa-tāḥ |
| त्रिशूलम् | त्रि · शू · लम् | tri-śū-lam |
| गर्भगृहम् | गर् · भ · गृ · हम् | gar-bha-gṛ-ham |
| क्षेत्रम् | क्षे · त्रम् | kṣe-tram |
| उत्तिष्ठ | उ · त्ति · ष्ठ | u-tti-ṣṭha |

## Unicode and How the Text Is Stored

Worth knowing if you ever type Sanskrit or search for it.

The text is stored in the order the sounds are said, not the order the marks
appear. कि is stored as क followed by the i sign, even though the sign prints
to the left. A conjunct is stored as consonant, virāma, consonant — क्ष is
क + virāma + ष — and the joined shape is the font's doing, not the text's.

This is why a naive search for क finds it inside क्ष, and why cursor movement
and deletion in Devanāgarī operate on the whole orthographic syllable rather
than on one stored character at a time.
