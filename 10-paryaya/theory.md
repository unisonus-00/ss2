# Stage 10: Paryāya — Synonyms

## Goal
Master synonyms (paryāyas) for key devotional concepts. Understand how divine epithets are built from attributes, and learn to generate names from qualities — the skill behind aṣṭottaraśatanāma (108 names).

## What is Paryāya?

**Paryāya** (पर्याय) means "synonym" or "alternative expression." Sanskrit is extraordinarily rich in synonyms because words are derived from roots: any quality or action associated with an entity can become its name.

## Why Synonyms Matter

1. **Poetic flexibility** — Different synonyms have different syllable counts and vowel patterns, critical for metrical composition
2. **Depth of understanding** — Each synonym reveals a different facet of the deity
3. **108 names tradition** — Aṣṭottaraśatanāmāvali are built from paryāyas
4. **Avadhāna skill** — Rapid synonym recall is essential for spontaneous composition

## How Epithets Are Built

### From Physical Attributes
- **nīla** (blue) + **kaṇṭha** (throat) = Nīlakaṇṭha (Śiva)
- **catur** (four) + **bhuja** (arm) = Caturbhuja (Viṣṇu)
- **tri** (three) + **netra** (eye) = Trinetra (Śiva)

### From Actions
- **√hṛ** (to remove) → Hara (the remover)
- **√pā** (to protect) → Gopāla (cow-protector = Kṛṣṇa)
- **√dhṛ** (to bear) → Gaṅgādhara (bearer of Gaṅgā)

### From Relationships
- **pārvata** (of the mountain) → Pārvatī (daughter of Parvata)
- **dāśarathi** (son of Daśaratha) → Rāma
- **umā + pati** → Umāpati (Śiva, husband of Umā)

## The 108 Names Forge Game

Given a deity, generate epithets by combining:
1. A quality + a body part → nīla-kaṇṭha
2. An action + an object → gaṅgā-dhara
3. A number + an attribute → tri-locana
4. A relationship + a title → umā-pati
5. A negation + a quality → a-mara (not dying = immortal)
