# Stage 7: Kāraka — Semantic Relations

## Goal
Understand the kāraka system — the deep semantic roles that map to vibhaktis — and build complete devotional sentences with multiple participants.

## What is Kāraka?

**Kāraka** (कारक) means "that which causes action." It is the Pāṇinian system for describing the semantic roles that nouns play in relation to a verb. While vibhakti is the surface form (ending), kāraka is the underlying meaning.

## The Six Kārakas

| Kāraka | Meaning | Typical Vibhakti | Example |
|--------|---------|------------------|---------|
| **Kartā** | Agent/doer | Prathamā (1st) | **bhaktaḥ** pūjayati (the devotee worships) |
| **Karma** | Object/patient | Dvitīyā (2nd) | bhaktaḥ **devam** pūjayati (worships the deity) |
| **Karaṇa** | Instrument/means | Tṛtīyā (3rd) | **puṣpeṇa** pūjayati (worships with a flower) |
| **Sampradāna** | Recipient/purpose | Caturthī (4th) | **śivāya** namaḥ (salutation to Śiva) |
| **Apādāna** | Source/origin | Pañcamī (5th) | **parvātat** nadī patati (river falls from mountain) |
| **Adhikaraṇa** | Location/locus | Saptamī (7th) | **mandire** pūjā (worship in the temple) |

Note: Ṣaṣṭhī (genitive, "of") is NOT a kāraka — it expresses sambandha (relation) rather than participation in the action.

## Kāraka in Devotional Sentences

### Full Sentence Analysis

**bhaktaḥ bhaktyā mandire śivāya puṣpam dadāti**
(The devotee gives a flower to Śiva in the temple with devotion)

| Word | Vibhakti | Kāraka | Role |
|------|---------|--------|------|
| bhaktaḥ | 1st | Kartā | agent — who gives |
| bhaktyā | 3rd | Karaṇa | instrument — by what means |
| mandire | 7th | Adhikaraṇa | location — where |
| śivāya | 4th | Sampradāna | recipient — to whom |
| puṣpam | 2nd | Karma | object — what is given |
| dadāti | verb | — | action — gives |

## Building Complex Devotional Sentences

### Pattern 1: Kartā + Karma + Kriyā (Subject + Object + Verb)
> bhaktaḥ devam namati — The devotee bows to the deity

### Pattern 2: + Karaṇa (adding instrument)
> bhaktaḥ puṣpeṇa devam pūjayati — The devotee worships the deity with a flower

### Pattern 3: + Sampradāna (adding recipient)
> bhaktaḥ śivāya puṣpam dadāti — The devotee gives a flower to Śiva

### Pattern 4: + Adhikaraṇa (adding location)
> bhaktaḥ mandire śivāya puṣpam dadāti — The devotee gives a flower to Śiva in the temple

### Pattern 5: + Karaṇa (adding manner)
> bhaktaḥ bhaktyā mandire śivāya puṣpam dadāti — with devotion, in the temple, to Śiva, a flower, the devotee gives

## The Pūjā Grammar Game

Express each step of pūjā as a full kāraka sentence:

1. **āvāhanam**: bhaktaḥ bhaktyā devam āvāhayati
   (The devotee invokes the deity with devotion)

2. **āsanam**: bhaktaḥ devāya āsanam arpayati
   (The devotee offers a seat to the deity)

3. **puṣpam**: bhaktaḥ devāya sundaram puṣpam dadāti
   (The devotee gives a beautiful flower to the deity)

4. **dhūpam**: bhaktaḥ dhūpena devam arcati
   (The devotee worships the deity with incense)

5. **naivedyam**: bhaktaḥ devāya naivedyam nivedayati
   (The devotee presents food to the deity)

## Tṛtīyā as Bhāva (Manner)

The instrumental case often expresses the emotional manner of worship:

| Expression | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| bhaktyā | with devotion |
| premaṇā | with love |
| śraddhayā | with faith |
| ānandena | with joy |
| namratayā | with humility |

> bhaktyā śivāya puṣpam dadāmi
> (With devotion I give a flower to Śiva)

## Word Order Freedom

Because kāraka relations are marked by endings, word order is flexible:
- śivāya puṣpam dadāmi
- puṣpam śivāya dadāmi
- dadāmi śivāya puṣpam
- puṣpam dadāmi śivāya

All mean the same thing. Choose the order that emphasizes what matters most (what comes first gets emphasis, what comes just before the verb gets focus).

## Milestone Check

Can you build 5 complete devotional sentences, each with at least 3 kārakas?
