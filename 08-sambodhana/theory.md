# Stage 8: Sambodhana — Direct Address

## Goal
Learn to speak directly to the deity — vocative case, imperatives, and spontaneous prayer. Move from talking *about* the divine to talking *to* the divine.

## What is Sambodhana?

**Sambodhana** (सम्बोधन) is the vocative case — the form used when calling or addressing someone directly. It is the grammar of prayer, invocation, and intimate conversation with the divine.

## Vocative Forms

### Masculine -a stems
- devaḥ → **he deva!** (O god!)
- śivaḥ → **he śiva!** (O Śiva!)
- rāmaḥ → **he rāma!** (O Rāma!)

Rule: Drop the -aḥ visarga, keep the -a. Prefix with **he** (हे).

### Feminine -ā stems
- durgā → **he durge!** (O Durgā!)
- gaṅgā → **he gaṅge!** (O Gaṅgā!)
- mālā → **he māle!**

Rule: -ā becomes -e.

### Feminine -ī stems
- devī → **he devi!** (O Goddess!)
- lakṣmī → **he lakṣmi!**
- pārvatī → **he pārvati!**

Rule: Long -ī shortens to -i.

### Masculine -u stems
- viṣṇuḥ → **he viṣṇo!** (O Viṣṇu!)
- guruḥ → **he guro!**

Rule: -uḥ becomes -o.

### Masculine -i stems
- agniḥ → **he agne!** (O Fire!)
- muniḥ → **he mune!**

Rule: -iḥ becomes -e.

## Imperatives (Loṭ-lakāra)

When addressing the deity, we use imperative verb forms:

### 2nd Person (requesting the deity to act)
| Verb | Imperative (2P sg) | Meaning |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| pā (protect) | **pāhi** | protect! |
| rakṣ (protect) | **rakṣa** | protect! |
| dā (give) | **dehi** | give! |
| kṛ (do/make) | **kuru** | do/make! |
| pra-sad (be gracious) | **prasīda** | be gracious! |
| kṣam (forgive) | **kṣamasva** | forgive! |
| nī (lead) | **naya** | lead! |
| śru (hear) | **śṛṇu** | hear/listen! |
| ā-gam (come) | **āgaccha** | come! |
| darśay (show) | **darśaya** | show! |

### 3rd Person (benedictive — "let X happen")
| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| pātu | let [deity] protect |
| rakṣatu | let [deity] protect |
| bhavatu | let it be |
| jayatu | let [deity] be victorious |
| nayatu | let [deity] lead |

## Building Spontaneous Prayers

### Structure 1: Address + Imperative
> he śiva! prasīda! — O Śiva, be gracious!
> he devi! rakṣa mām! — O Goddess, protect me!
> he kṛṣṇa! pāhi mām! — O Kṛṣṇa, save me!

### Structure 2: Address + Imperative + Object
> he rāma! dehi me bhaktim — O Rāma, give me devotion!
> he devi! dehi me jñānam — O Goddess, give me knowledge!
> he guru! naya mām — O Guru, lead me!

### Structure 3: Address + Description + Imperative
> he karuṇāmaya śiva! prasīda! — O compassionate Śiva, be gracious!
> he jagadambike! rakṣa mām! — O Mother of the world, protect me!

### Structure 4: Multiple Requests
> he devi! pāhi mām, rakṣa mām, naya mām!
> O Goddess, save me, protect me, lead me!

## Key Pronoun: mām (me)

When praying, you often need "me" — this is **mām** (माम्), the accusative of aham:

| Case | Form | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| Nominative | aham | I |
| Accusative | mām | me (object) |
| Instrumental | mayā | by me |
| Dative | mahyam / me | to/for me |
| Genitive | mama / me | my, of me |
| Locative | mayi | in me |

## The Particle "namaḥ"

**namaḥ** takes the dative (caturthī), not the vocative:
- śivāya namaḥ (salutation TO Śiva) — correct
- he śiva! namaḥ — mixing vocative address with namaḥ (also used)

## Classical Prayer Phrases

| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| he śiva prasīda | O Śiva, be gracious |
| he devi rakṣa mām | O Goddess, protect me |
| tvaṃ eva mātā ca pitā tvaṃ eva | You alone are mother and father |
| tvaṃ eva sarvaṃ mama deva deva | You alone are everything, O God of gods |
| śaraṇam prapadye | I take refuge |
| tvām eva śaraṇam | You alone are refuge |
| kṛpāṃ kuru | show mercy |
| bhayāt trāhi | save from fear |

## Milestone Check

Can you compose a spontaneous 4-line prayer to your iṣṭadevatā using vocative + imperatives?

### 🧱 New Bricks Learned

| Brick Type | Examples | Snaps onto... |
|------------|----------|---------------|
| Vocative brick | he rāma!, he devi!, he śiva!, he guro!, he agne! | Any sentence — this brick "addresses" the listener directly; snaps onto imperative verbs (pāhi, dehi, rakṣa) and kāraka sentences from Stage 7 to turn statements into prayers |

**Try snapping**: Take a full kāraka sentence from Stage 7 (e.g., "bhaktaḥ bhaktyā mandire devāya puṣpam dadāti"), replace the third-person kartā with "aham," swap the verb to first person, and snap a vocative brick onto the front — now it is a direct prayer instead of a description.
