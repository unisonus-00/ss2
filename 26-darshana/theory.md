# Stage 26: Darśana — Philosophical Expression

## Goal
Describe the deity as consciousness, time, śakti, and other philosophical tattvas. Make philosophy become poetry.

## Core Philosophical Concepts for Poetic Use

### Brahman / Ātman
> tvam eva sarvam — you alone are everything
> sacchidānanda-rūpa — whose nature is being-consciousness-bliss
> nityam, śuddham, buddham, muktam — eternal, pure, awake, free

### Māyā / Prakṛti
> māyādhīśa — lord of illusion
> prakṛti-puruṣa — nature and consciousness
> nāma-rūpa-vilāsa — the play of name and form

### Kāla (Time)
> kālātīta — beyond time
> mahākāla — great time (Śiva as time itself)
> kālasya kālaḥ — the time of time (death of death)

### Śakti (Power)
> cit-śakti — consciousness-power
> icchā-jñāna-kriyā — will, knowledge, action (the three śaktis)
> parā-śaktiḥ — supreme power

### Pañca-kṛtya (Five Acts)
> sṛṣṭi (creation), sthiti (maintenance), saṃhāra (dissolution), tirodhāna (concealment), anugraha (grace)

## Philosophical Praise Patterns

### Pattern 1: Neti-neti (not this, not this)
> na strī na puruṣaḥ, na ca napuṃsakaḥ
> na sthūlaḥ na sūkṣmaḥ, na dṛśyaḥ na śrāvyaḥ
> sarvasya sākṣī, sarvasya adhiṣṭhānam

### Pattern 2: Paradox as Praise
> aṇoḥ aṇīyān mahato mahīyān (smaller than the small, greater than the great)
> dūre ca antike ca (far and near)
> sarva-gataḥ acalaḥ (all-pervading yet unmoving)

### Pattern 3: Tattva Identification
> tvaṃ ākāśaḥ, tvaṃ vāyuḥ, tvaṃ agniḥ, tvaṃ āpaḥ, tvaṃ pṛthivī
> tvaṃ sarvam idam — you are all this

### Pattern 4: Philosophical Epithets
> caitanya-rūpa (consciousness-formed)
> ānanda-ghana (mass of bliss)
> jñāna-svarūpa (knowledge-natured)
> sat-cit-ānanda (being-consciousness-bliss)
> svayam-prakāśa (self-luminous)

## The Game: "Praise Through Tattva"

Instead of praising a deity's form (eyes, arms), praise through philosophical categories:

> he śiva! tvaṃ caitanyam, tvaṃ ākāśaḥ
> tvaṃ kālaḥ, tvaṃ kālātītaḥ
> tvayi sṛṣṭiḥ, tvayi sthitiḥ, tvayi layaḥ
> tvam advayam brahma, nānyat kiñcit

## Milestone Check
Can you praise your deity philosophically — describing them as consciousness, time, and śakti — making it sound like poetry, not a textbook?
