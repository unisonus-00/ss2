# Stage 33: Aprastuta-prasaṅga — Wit and Context Switching

## Goal
Respond intelligently to interruptions and distractions while retaining your ongoing devotional composition. Train resilient attention.

## What is Aprastuta-prasaṅga?

**Aprastuta** = irrelevant. **Prasaṅga** = topic/occasion. In avadhāna, a pṛcchaka (questioner) deliberately asks off-topic, witty, or tricky questions to distract you from your compositions. You must answer intelligently AND return to your verses without losing your place.

## The Skill Set

### 1. Rapid Wit (Pratibhā)
Answer the distraction cleverly — ideally in Sanskrit, with humor or depth.

### 2. Context Preservation
While answering, hold your unfinished verse in a "background thread."

### 3. Graceful Return
After answering, smoothly resume composition as if uninterrupted.

## Types of Distracting Questions

| Type | Example | How to Answer |
|------|---------|--------------|
| Factual | "How many sons did Daśaratha have?" | Quick factual answer |
| Riddle | "What has eyes but cannot see?" | Creative answer (needle? potato? māyā?) |
| Philosophical | "If Brahman is nirguṇa, how can it create?" | Brief philosophical response |
| Trick | "Is a coconut a fruit or a nut?" | Note the trick, answer wisely |
| Provocative | "Your last pāda was grammatically wrong!" | Stay composed, correct if needed |
| Computational | "What is 17 × 23?" | Quick mental math |

## Training Exercises

### Exercise 1: Compose + Interrupt
- Begin composing a stotra
- After each pāda, answer a random question (have a friend ask, or use flashcards)
- Return to composition

### Exercise 2: Dual-Task
- Recite a known stotra while simultaneously counting backward from 100 by 7s
- The stotra tests verbal memory; counting tests numerical attention

### Exercise 3: Witty Response Practice
Given: "Why is Gaṇeśa's vehicle a mouse?"
Answer: "The mouse represents the mind — small, quick, always gnawing. Gaṇeśa, lord of wisdom, rides the mind, meaning he has mastered it."

## Attention Management

### The "Pause and Resume" Technique
1. When interrupted, mentally "bookmark" your composition: note the first word of the pāda you were about to compose
2. Give full attention to the question
3. Answer it completely
4. Retrieve your bookmark and continue

### The "Background Hum" Technique
Keep the rhythm of your unfinished verse humming mentally while answering the question. Your mouth speaks the answer; your inner ear holds the meter.

## Key Principle
The avadhānī never appears flustered. Even under distraction, maintain composure (dhairya). A witty response to an interruption is itself an ornament to the performance.

## Milestone Check
Can you compose a verse, answer 3 unrelated questions mid-composition, and complete the verse correctly?
