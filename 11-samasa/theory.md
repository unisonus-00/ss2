# Stage 11: Samāsa — Compounds

## Goal
Master Sanskrit compound formation — the engine behind divine epithets, philosophical terms, and poetic expression. Learn to build and decompose compounds, and invent grammatically valid new epithets.

## What is Samāsa?

**Samāsa** (समास) means "throwing together" — the joining of two or more words into a single compound. Sanskrit compounds can be enormously long, but they follow strict rules.

## The Four Major Types

### 1. Tatpuruṣa (Determinative) — "his man"
The first member modifies the second. The second member is the head word.

**Pattern:** modifier + head (relation between members = a vibhakti)

| Subtype | Vibhakti | Example | Analysis | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|----------|---------|
| Dvitīyā-tat. | 2nd | grāma-gata | grāmam gataḥ | gone to the village |
| Tṛtīyā-tat. | 3rd | deva-datta | devena dattaḥ | given by god |
| Caturthī-tat. | 4th | yajña-śālā | yajñāya śālā | hall for sacrifice |
| Pañcamī-tat. | 5th | svarga-patita | svargāt patitaḥ | fallen from heaven |
| Ṣaṣṭhī-tat. | 6th | rāja-putra | rājñaḥ putraḥ | king's son |
| Saptamī-tat. | 7th | vana-vāsa | vane vāsaḥ | dwelling in forest |

### 2. Dvandva (Copulative) — "and"
Both members are equal; the compound means "A and B."

| Example | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| rāma-lakṣmaṇau | Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa (dual) |
| dharma-artha-kāma-mokṣāḥ | dharma, artha, kāma, and mokṣa (pl) |
| śiva-pārvatī | Śiva and Pārvatī |
| deva-asurāḥ | gods and demons |

### 3. Bahuvrīhi (Possessive/Exocentric) — "much rice"
The compound describes something/someone *outside* itself. "The one who has X."

| Example | Literal | Actual Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| nīla-kaṇṭha | blue-throat | he whose throat is blue (= Śiva) |
| catur-bhuja | four-arm | he who has four arms (= Viṣṇu) |
| padma-nābha | lotus-navel | he whose navel has a lotus (= Viṣṇu) |
| daśa-ānana | ten-face | he who has ten faces (= Rāvaṇa) |
| mahā-bāhu | great-arm | he who has great arms |
| pīta-ambara | yellow-cloth | he who wears yellow cloth (= Viṣṇu) |

This is the most common type for deity epithets.

### 4. Avyayībhāva (Indeclinable/Adverbial)
The first member is an indeclinable, and the whole compound becomes adverbial.

| Example | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| yathā-śakti | according to ability |
| prati-dinam | every day |
| upa-kūlam | near the bank |
| nir-bhayam | fearlessly |

## Special Compound Types

### Karmadhāraya (Appositional Tatpuruṣa)
Both members refer to the same entity — adjective + noun or noun + noun in apposition.

| Example | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| mahā-deva | great-god (Mahādeva) |
| nīla-utpala | blue-lotus |
| parama-ātman | supreme-self |
| sat-guru | true-teacher |

### Dvigu (Numeral Tatpuruṣa)
First member is a number.

| Example | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| tri-loka | three worlds |
| pañca-bhūta | five elements |
| sapta-ṛṣi | seven sages |
| daśa-āvatāra | ten incarnations |

## The Build-a-Deity Game

Create valid epithets by combining components:

| Component A | Component B | Compound | Type | Deity |
|------------|------------|----------|------|-------|
| gaṅgā | dhara | gaṅgādhara | Bahu. | Śiva |
| tri | netra | trinetra | Bahu. | Śiva |
| vana | mālī | vanamālī | Bahu. | Viṣṇu |
| jagat | ambā | jagadambā | Ṣaṣṭhī-tat. | Devī |
| mahā | lakṣmī | mahālakṣmī | Karm. | Devī |
| cakra | pāṇi | cakrapāṇi | Bahu. | Viṣṇu |
| mṛga | aṅka | mṛgāṅka | Bahu. | Moon |

## Sandhi in Compounds

When words join, sandhi rules apply at the junction:
- jagat + ambā → **jagadambā** (t + a → da)
- mahā + īśvara → **maheśvara** (ā + ī → e)
- nīla + utpala → **nīlotpala** (a + u → o)
- vana + oṣadhi → **vanauṣadhi** (a + o → au)
