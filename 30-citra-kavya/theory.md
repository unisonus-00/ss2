# Stage 30: Citra-kāvya — Stacked Constraints

## Goal
Compose verse under multiple simultaneous constraints: meter + deity + rasa + required word + prohibition. This is the culmination of all previous skills working together.

## What is Citra-kāvya?

**Citra** = picture, wonder. **Kāvya** = poetry. Citra-kāvya is poetry composed under extraordinary constraints — visual patterns, phonological restrictions, or multiple simultaneous requirements.

## Constraint Types

1. **Chandas** — specific meter required
2. **Viṣaya** — specific deity/topic required
3. **Rasa** — specific emotional flavor required
4. **Dattapada** — required word(s) that must appear
5. **Niṣiddhākṣarī** — forbidden letter(s)
6. **Alaṅkāra** — specific figure of speech required
7. **Vibhakti** — specific case(s) must dominate

## The Yantra Verse Game

You are given ALL constraints simultaneously:

**Example Challenge:**
- Meter: anuṣṭubh (8 syllables per pāda)
- Deity: Śiva
- Rasa: karuṇa (compassion)
- Required word: sindhu (ocean)
- Forbidden letter: na
- Required alaṅkāra: upamā (simile)

**Solution Process:**

1. Check: which words about Śiva contain "na"? → nandī ✗, naṭarāja ✗, candra ✗... use hara, śambhu, tryakṣa
2. "sindhu" must appear → karuṇā-sindhu iva (simile + required word + rasa!)
3. Compose in 8-syllable pādas
4. Verify all constraints met

Let's check: if it's the akṣara "na" specifically = the sound /na/, then "ni" is a different akṣara. Typically in avadhāna, the specific akṣara is forbidden.]

Let me be safe:
> dīro'smi he dayālu hara (8?)

## Multi-Constraint Checklist

Before composing, lay out all constraints:
```
☐ Meter: ___
☐ Deity: ___
☐ Rasa: ___
☐ Required word(s): ___
☐ Forbidden letter(s): ___
☐ Required alaṅkāra: ___
☐ Other: ___
```

After composing, verify each:
```
☐ All pādas correct syllable count?
☐ Deity named/described?
☐ Rasa consistent?
☐ Required word present?
☐ No forbidden letter anywhere?
☐ Required alaṅkāra present?
```

## Classical Citra-kāvya Forms

### Sarvatobhadra (palindrome in all directions)
A verse that reads the same forward and backward.

### Cakrabandha (wheel poem)
Syllables arranged in a wheel pattern.

### Padmabandha (lotus poem)
Syllables arranged in lotus-petal pattern.

(These visual forms are advanced — the constraint-stacking approach is the practical foundation.)
