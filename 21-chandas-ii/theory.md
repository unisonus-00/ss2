# Stage 21: Chandas II — Anuṣṭubh (Śloka)

## Goal
Master the anuṣṭubh/śloka meter and compose spontaneous verses in it.

## Anuṣṭubh Structure

4 pādas × 8 syllables = 32 syllables per verse. Two pādas form a half-verse (ardha-śloka).

```
pāda a (8) | pāda b (8)    ← first half
pāda c (8) | pāda d (8)    ← second half
```

## The Rules

The anuṣṭubh is **not** strictly fixed in all positions. The key constraints:

### Even pādas (b and d):
- Position 5 must be **laghu** (∪)
- Position 6 must be **guru** (–)
- Position 7 must be **guru** (–) [in pāda b] or **laghu** (∪) [in pāda d is flexible]
- Pattern at 5-6-7: typically ∪ – – (b) and ∪ – ∪ (d)

### Odd pādas (a and c):
- Position 5 should **not** be ∪ – ∪ (ja-gaṇa at 5-6-7 is avoided)
- Otherwise quite free

### Summary of key constraint:
```
Pāda b: × × × × ∪ – – ×    (positions 5-6-7 = ∪ – –)
Pāda d: × × × × ∪ – ∪ ×    (positions 5-6-7 = ∪ – ∪)
```
(× = free, but certain patterns preferred)

## Yati (Caesura)

A natural word-break (yati) typically falls after the 4th syllable in each pāda.

## Classical Examples

### Bhagavadgītā 2.47:
```
kar-ma-ṇy-e-vā-dhi-kā-ras-te    (a: 8 syllables)
mā pha-le-ṣu ka-dā-ca-na         (b: 8, pos 5-7: ∪ – ∪... actually this is flexible)
mā kar-ma-pha-la-he-tur bhūr       (c: 8)
mā te saṅ-go'-stv a-kar-ma-ṇi     (d: 8)
```

### Simple anuṣṭubh:
```
na-mā-mi śi-vaṃ śān-taṃ de-   (a)
vaṃ tri-lo-ca-naṃ ha-ram       (b)
nī-la-kaṇ-ṭhaṃ ga-ṅgā-dha-    (c)
raṃ na-ṭa-rā-jaṃ na-mā-my-a-ham (d)
```

## Composition Process

### Step 1: Choose your message
"I bow to peaceful Śiva who has three eyes"

### Step 2: List key words with syllable counts
namāmi (3), śivam (2), śāntam (2), trilocanam (4), devam (2)

### Step 3: Arrange into 8-syllable pādas
Try: namāmi śivaṃ śāntam (7... need 1 more)
→ namāmi śivaṃ śāntaṃ tam (8? adjust...)

### Step 4: Check constraints on positions 5-6-7

### Step 5: Adjust with synonyms if needed
Replace "trilocanam" (4) with "trinetram" (3) or "tryambakam" (3) if needed for fit.

## Practice Technique

1. Take any simple devotional sentence
2. Restate it to fit 8 syllables per half-line
3. Use synonyms to adjust syllable count
4. Check laghu/guru at positions 5-6-7 in even pādas
5. Recite aloud — it should flow naturally

## The Śloka Offering Game

Compose one śloka (4 pādas) as a daily offering to your iṣṭadevatā.

## Milestone Check
Can you compose a spontaneous anuṣṭubh śloka on a given devotional theme?
